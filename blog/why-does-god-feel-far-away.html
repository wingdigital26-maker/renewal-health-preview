Why Does God Feel So Far Away? Walking Through a Spiritual Dry Season

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 15, 2026

God can feel far away for many reasons: exhaustion, grief, long stress, disappointment, or a season when prayer feels like talking to the ceiling. Feeling distant is not the same as being abandoned. Often the soul is tired and the body is on alert, so the nearness that is still there becomes hard to sense.

Why does God feel far away when I need Him most?

Hard seasons are usually when we reach for God hardest, and they are also the seasons when our capacity to feel anything is lowest. Grief, illness, caregiving, conflict, and burnout drain the same reserves we use to feel connection, comfort, and joy.

I have sat with many people who whispered this question with shame, as if admitting it were a failure of faith. It is not. Some of the most honest prayers in Scripture are prayers of distance. David asked, "How long wilt thou hide thy face from me?" The question itself is a form of reaching.

Can stress and exhaustion make God feel distant?

Yes, and this is where the body matters more than most of us were taught. In my work I describe the person as Spirit, Soul, and Body, and the body is the most honest witness of the three. When your nervous system is locked in survival mode, it narrows your attention to threats. It is built to scan for danger, not to notice quiet presence.

In that state, feelings of warmth and closeness are often the first to fade. You may still believe every word you believed before, yet feel flat when you pray, worship, or read. That flatness is frequently a sign of depletion, not of disconnection. My post on why you feel emotionally numb explores that protective shutdown more fully.

This is why I so often say the healing order is to stabilize the Body, soften the Soul, and awaken the Spirit. A body that finally feels safe often notices God again before the mind has figured out why.

Is a spiritual dry season normal?

Very normal. People of faith across the centuries have written about seasons of dryness, silence, and waiting. Many describe them later as times when roots went deeper, even though nothing seemed to grow above ground. I wrote about this kind of hidden growth in when defeat becomes a doorway.

A dry season can also carry unspoken questions. Disappointment with God, prayers that seemed unanswered, or old teaching that made Him feel harsh can all build a quiet wall. Naming those things honestly, to Him and to a safe person, is often the first crack of light.

How do I feel close to God again?

There is no formula, and you cannot force a feeling. But you can make space for nearness to be noticed again.

  1. Start with your body. Slow your breath, lengthen your exhale, and let your shoulders drop before you pray. Calm makes room for connection.
  2. Pray honestly, even briefly. "I cannot feel You right now" is a real prayer. So is silence.
  3. Borrow words when you have none. Read a Psalm aloud. Many were written from exactly this place.
  4. Look for small signs of goodness. A kind word, morning light, a moment of ease. I call these glimmers, and noticing them gently retrains a weary system to sense safety.
  5. Do not walk alone. Let a trusted friend, pastor, or practitioner carry some of the weight with you.
  6. Rest without guilt. Sometimes the most faithful thing you can do is sleep.

If listening is the part that feels hardest, my post on how to hear God's voice when fear is loud offers gentle practices. And if you want to go deeper into how Spirit, Soul, and Body work together, I wrote about it at length in my book, Nothing Missing, Nothing Broken.

When should I reach out for extra support?

A season of spiritual dryness can overlap with depression, grief, or burnout. If the heaviness lasts most days for more than two weeks, or if you lose interest in things you used to love, please talk with your physician or a licensed counselor. The National Institute of Mental Health explains common signs of depression in plain language. If you are having thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 to reach the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline right away.

My emotional overwhelm and nervous system support can walk alongside that care, helping your body feel safe enough to rest and reconnect, never instead of it.

Pause for Reflection

Sit somewhere quiet. Let your breath slow, and rest one hand over your heart.

Ask gently: When did I last feel close to God, and what was my body like then? What has my soul been carrying since? What would it mean to believe that He is near, even if I cannot feel it today?

"The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit" (Psalm 34:18). Nearness is His promise, not your performance.

FAQ

Does feeling far from God mean I have lost my faith?

No. Feelings rise and fall with sleep, stress, grief, and health. Faith often looks like continuing to reach even when you feel nothing in return.

Why can I not feel God's presence when I pray?

Exhaustion and a nervous system on alert can mute feelings of warmth and closeness. Calming the body first, then praying simply and honestly, often helps.

How long does a spiritual dry season last?

It varies from person to person. Many people find that rest, honest prayer, and supportive community help the season shift over time.
