How Do You Stop Being a Perfectionist?

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · September 26, 2026

You stop being a perfectionist by treating it as a safety strategy rather than a flaw to fix. Notice it without judgment, trace where the rule first came from, then give your body small, repeated experiences of "good enough" being safe. Trying harder to stop trying harder rarely works. Feeling safer usually does.

Why am I such a perfectionist?

Most perfectionism is a bargain made a long time ago. It sounds something like this: "If I am perfect, no one will criticize my presence."

Often in childhood, love or approval felt conditional. A child's nervous system is brilliant at adapting, and it concluded that flawlessness was the price of safety.

In my book I call this the Perfectionist Script: "If I am not perfect, I am not safe." And inside every script there is a hidden love. This one says, "I will be so perfect that you cannot help but love and approve of me." That is not vanity. That is a child trying very hard to stay connected.

It often travels in a family, too. If your parents or grandparents lived by the same rule, you may have absorbed it through the atmosphere of your home rather than any spoken lesson. My post on survival scripts walks through the other rules that tend to travel with it.

What does perfectionism do to your body?

It keeps you braced.

A perfectionist nervous system is always scanning for the mistake that has not happened yet. The jaw tightens, the shoulders creep up, the breath stays shallow, and rest never quite arrives because there is always one more thing to check.

That is why I do not treat perfectionism as purely a mindset problem. The standard lives in the mind, but the fear lives in the body. Until the body feels a little safer, the mind will keep defending the old standard, and the work I do on emotional overwhelm and the nervous system starts right there.

Is perfectionism connected to procrastination and avoidance?

Very often, yes. Perfectionism rarely travels alone. I think of it as one strand in a larger web, and two of its closest companions are:

  • Avoidance: "If I stay away, I will not have to defend my right to be here." Putting off the task you care most about is often perfectionism in disguise, because a thing never started can never be judged.
  • Emotional suppression: "If I do not feel, I will not accidentally reveal how much I am hurting." Keeping a flawless surface can mean keeping feelings out of sight, even from yourself.

Seen as a web, procrastination stops looking like laziness and starts looking like protection.

For many neurodivergent adults, perfectionism grows out of years of masking, working twice as hard to appear "normal" and bracing for the moment someone notices a difference. If that is familiar, my support for neurodivergent adults and families begins from acceptance rather than correction.

How do you stop being a perfectionist, step by step?

Patterns loosen when the nervous system feels safe enough to choose a different path. Not by trying harder. Here is the gentle sequence I teach.

1. Notice without judgment. Instead of asking "Why am I still like this?", which is a question rooted in shame, ask "Where did this strand come from?" You are not the pattern. You are the one observing it.

2. Trace the thread. When the urge to redo, recheck, or over-prepare rises, pause and ask: "What am I afraid will happen if this is only good enough?" The answer is usually older than the task in front of you.

3. Take the pause. One slow breath before you reach for the fix. That small gap is where choice lives.

4. Anchor in your body. Feel your feet on the floor. Let your shoulders drop. Unclench your jaw. You are showing your body that you can tolerate imperfection without danger.

5. Tell the truth. Say it to yourself, quietly: "That was then, this is now. I am safe here."

6. Make micro-choices. Send the email with one small typo. Leave one dish in the sink. Ask for help once. Each of these is a small rehearsal of a new pathway.

What is the difference between high standards and perfectionism?

High standards come from care. Perfectionism comes from fear.

A healthy standard can bend. It lets you finish, learn, and rest. Perfectionism cannot bend, because it is not really about the work. It is about staying safe from criticism, rejection, or shame. A simple test: when you fall short, do you feel disappointed, or do you feel in danger? Disappointment is information. Danger is the old script talking.

When should you get extra support?

If perfectionism comes with anxiety that will not settle, compulsive checking you cannot stop, disordered eating, or low mood that lingers, please speak with your physician or a licensed mental health professional. The National Institute of Mental Health offers a clear guide to caring for your mental health and when to reach out.

Pause for reflection

Place a hand over your heart or your abdomen. Take a long, slow breath.

Ask yourself softly: What pattern in my life feels like a strand of a much larger, older web? What if I do not have to fix it, only understand where it came from and show it that it is safe to let go?

"My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness" (2 Corinthians 12:9).

You are not tangled. You are unraveling. The process is gentle, and it is working.

FAQ

Is perfectionism a mental illness?

Perfectionism on its own is a pattern, not a diagnosis. When it comes with persistent anxiety, compulsions, or low mood, it is worth talking with a licensed professional.

Can perfectionism come from my family?

Often, yes. Rules like "never make a mistake" can be absorbed through a home's atmosphere and passed down without ever being spoken.

Why do I procrastinate if I am a perfectionist?

Because a task never started can never be judged. Procrastination is frequently perfectionism protecting you from the fear of falling short.

Where can I start if I want help?

Try the six steps above for one week, starting with the pause. If you would like to explore this together, you can start a conversation through the contact page.
