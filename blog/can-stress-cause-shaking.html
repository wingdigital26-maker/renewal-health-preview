Can Stress Cause Shaking? Why Your Body Trembles

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 21, 2026

Yes, stress and anxiety can cause shaking or trembling, most often in the hands, legs, or voice. A surge of stress hormones gets the muscles ready to move, and that extra energy can show up as a tremble. Shaking that is new, ongoing, or comes with other symptoms deserves a visit with your physician.

Why do I shake when I am nervous or stressed?

When your nervous system senses a threat, it releases adrenaline to help you act fast. The heart speeds up, blood moves toward the big muscles, and those muscles tighten, ready to run or push. If there is nowhere to run, because the threat is an email or a difficult conversation, that ready energy has to go somewhere. Often it comes out as a tremble.

You may notice shaky hands when you hold a cup, a quiver in your voice, wobbly legs, or an inner trembling no one else can see. Many people also feel their hands and feet go cold at the same moment, which I explain in my post on whether stress can cause cold hands and feet. Both come from the same shift of blood and energy toward the core.

Why do I shake after a stressful event is over?

This surprises many people. You hold it together through the hard moment, then start shaking once you are safe in the car or at home. That is not weakness. It is often the body discharging the energy it gathered to protect you.

I see this often in the people I sit with. A mother tells me she stayed steady through her child's emergency room visit, then shook for an hour afterward and felt embarrassed about it. I call symptoms messengers rather than enemies. That after-shake is often your body saying, "We made it. I can let go now." Gentle movement can help it move through, and my post on somatic exercises to release tension offers simple ways to start.

Can anxiety cause shaking all the time?

When the alarm gear stays switched on for weeks or months, some people notice a low, steady shakiness much of the day, or feel jittery on waking. Caffeine, skipped meals, and short sleep can all add to it.

Shaking also has many causes that are not stress. Low blood sugar, thyroid changes, some medications, too much caffeine, and neurological conditions can all cause a tremor. The MedlinePlus page on tremor is a helpful, plain-language overview. Your physician is the right person to sort this out, and natural-first support works best alongside that care, never instead of it.

How can I stop shaking from anxiety?

Think of these as small signals of safety, offered alongside any care your physician recommends.

  1. Let it move, gently. Shake out your hands, roll your shoulders, or walk for a few minutes. Fighting the tremble often tightens it.
  2. Lengthen the exhale. A slow out-breath tells the body the emergency is over.
  3. Press into the ground. Plant your feet, press your palms together, or lean against a wall. Firm, steady pressure can feel grounding.
  4. Warm up. A warm drink, a blanket, or warm water over your hands can help the body feel safe again.
  5. Eat something steady. Going too long without food can make shakiness worse, so a simple snack with some protein may help.
  6. Ease off stimulants. Notice how coffee and energy drinks affect your shaking.

When should I see a doctor about shaking?

Please see your physician if shaking is new and does not go away, if it gets worse over time, if it affects your handwriting, eating, or daily tasks, or if it comes with weight loss, heat intolerance, or a racing heart. Seek emergency care right away for shaking with confusion, a high fever, or fainting, after a head injury, or with sudden weakness, numbness, or drooping on one side of the face or body.

Pause for Reflection

Rest both hands on your lap. Let your feet feel the floor. Let one slow breath out.

Ask gently: What have I been holding steady for everyone else? Where could I let my body finish the shake it has been saving?

"Be still, and know that I am God" (Psalm 46:10). Stillness is not something you force. It is something your body is allowed to return to.

FAQ

Is it normal to shake when you are anxious?

Yes, trembling is a common part of the body's stress response. If it happens often or does not settle, please talk with your physician.

Can stress cause internal shaking?

Many people describe an inner tremble or buzzing that no one else can see. It is often linked to an alert nervous system, but it can have other causes, so mention it to your physician.

Why do my legs shake when I am nervous?

Adrenaline readies the large leg muscles to move, so legs often tremble first. Walking or gently shaking them out can help.
