Rest and Digest: Why You Cannot Heal Your Gut While Bracing

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · August 24, 2026

If your digestion has felt off for a while and nothing seems to fully settle it, the missing piece may not be what you are eating. It may be the state your nervous system is in while you eat it.

The state your body needs to digest well

Your body has two very different modes it can run in. One is built for facing a threat, alert, tense, ready to react. The other is built for rest, repair, and yes, digestion. Your body was never designed to do both well at the same time. When it is busy bracing for something, digestion is one of the first systems it deprioritizes.

This is not a flaw. It is a survival design that makes sense in a genuine emergency. The trouble is when that alert state becomes the body's default setting, even when nothing dangerous is actually happening.

What "bracing" looks like day to day

Bracing does not always look dramatic. It can look like eating lunch at your desk while answering messages, rushing through a meal between errands, or sitting down to eat while your mind is still running through everything else on your plate that day, so to speak. None of that looks like stress on the surface. To your nervous system, it can register the same way.

Why this matters for gut symptoms

When the body is in an alert state, blood flow and energy shift toward the systems built for reacting, and away from the slower, quieter work of digestion. Over time, a nervous system that rarely gets to fully downshift can leave digestion running under less-than-ideal conditions on a regular basis, even when the food itself is not the problem.

This is part of why some people can change their diet significantly and still feel unsettled. The food changed, but the state the body was digesting in did not.

What actually helps

The goal is not to eliminate stress from your life, that is not realistic for anyone. The goal is helping your body find its way back to a rest-and-digest state more easily and more often, especially around meals. A few gentle starting points:

  • Taking even three slow breaths before you start eating, not to be perfect about it, just to signal to your body that it is safe to shift gears
  • Eating without a screen or a task competing for your attention, when that is realistic for your day
  • Noticing if you are eating quickly because you are actually rushed, or out of habit
  • Treating a meal as a small pause rather than one more task to get through

None of these are quick fixes. They are small, repeatable signals that help the nervous system settle, which gives digestion a better chance to do its job.

When to loop in your doctor

If you are dealing with persistent digestive symptoms, unexplained changes, or anything that concerns you, please talk with your physician. Nervous system support is a complementary piece of the picture, not a substitute for medical evaluation of digestive symptoms, and it works best alongside your doctor's care.

FAQ

Can nervous system work alone fix digestive issues?

No single approach should be expected to fix everything on its own, and no responsible practitioner promises a specific result. Nervous system support is one piece of a fuller picture, best used alongside medical care.

Is this the same as saying stress causes all gut problems?

No. Digestive symptoms can have many contributing factors, and a persistent or concerning symptom always deserves a conversation with your doctor. This is about one contributing piece, not the whole explanation.

How long does it take to notice a difference?

This varies a great deal from person to person, and no two timelines look the same. Consistency with small, repeatable practices tends to matter more than any single big change.

Pause for reflection

Psalm 23 speaks of being led beside quiet waters, a picture of rest that is offered, not forced. Your digestion may be asking for a version of that same quiet, a few unhurried minutes where your body is allowed to simply be at rest instead of on guard.
