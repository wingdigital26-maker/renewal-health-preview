How Do You Build Self-Confidence When You Still Feel Shaky?

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · September 27, 2026

You build self-confidence less by becoming fearless and more by gathering proof that you can handle what comes. Confidence grows when your body feels safe enough to stop shrinking, your inner voice tells the truth, and you take small brave steps while still shaky. It is not a personality you lack. It is a capacity you can grow.

What is self-confidence, really?

Most of us picture confidence as loud certainty: the person who never hesitates, never doubts, and fills every room.

I see it differently. In my book, I describe confidence as the quiet knowledge that you are capable of walking the path, not because it is easy, but because you know how to stay present when it is hard, repair when you have made a mistake, and return to center when you have drifted.

That kind of confidence does not require you to stop feeling nervous. It only requires you to trust that nervousness is not the end of the story.

Why do I have such low self-confidence?

Low confidence is rarely about ability. It is usually about safety.

If you grew up in a home where you were criticized, overlooked, or told you were "too much," your nervous system learned a clever strategy: stay small, stay quiet, do not stand out. Shrinking kept you out of trouble. It was protection, and it worked.

As an adult, that same strategy can show up as apologizing for your opinions, deferring to others, or talking yourself out of opportunities before anyone else can. My post on the apology reflex looks closely at one of the most common ways this shows up.

None of this means something is wrong with you. It means your body is still following instructions it was given a long time ago.

Does confidence start in the body or the mind?

Both, but I usually begin with the body.

A braced body sends the brain a constant signal that you are under threat. It is very hard to feel confident while your shoulders are up by your ears and your breath is stuck high in your chest. When the body softens, the mind often follows.

The inner narrative matters just as much. The old story says "I am too much." The truer story says "I am designed with purpose." If your inner voice tends toward harshness, my post on how to change negative self-talk walks through catching and replacing the old script. This is the whole-person approach behind all of my services: body, soul, and spirit working together rather than one at a time.

How do you build self-confidence, step by step?

Here is the gentle sequence I teach. It is slow on purpose.

1. Notice where you shrink. For one week, simply notice the moments you make yourself smaller: the swallowed opinion, the automatic "sorry," the chair at the back of the room. Do not fix anything yet. Just notice.

2. Soften your frame. Before a moment that usually shrinks you, feel your feet on the floor, lengthen your spine, and take one slow breath into your belly. Posture is not a trick. It is a message to your nervous system.

3. Speak one true sentence. Share one real opinion in a safe setting. Not a speech. One sentence.

4. Let yourself be shaky. You do not have to wait until you feel strong. Moving forward while still feeling soft is, I believe, the bravest kind of living.

5. Collect your proof. At the end of each day, write down one moment you showed up. Confidence is built from evidence, and your body needs to see it.

6. Practice returning. When you slip back into shrinking, do not scold yourself. Breathe, check in, and begin again. Returning is part of confidence, not a failure of it.

Is tenderness a weakness?

No. True courage often looks like tenderness. It is the bravery to stay soft when life has given you every reason to harden.

Some people try to build confidence by becoming tough, loud, or defended. But armor is not confidence. It is another form of bracing. Real confidence lets you stay kind, stay open, and still hold your ground. When you no longer need to convince anyone of your worth, you gain enormous energy for what matters most: love, presence, purpose, and peace.

When should you get extra support?

If low confidence comes with persistent low mood, anxiety that interferes with daily life, or thoughts of worthlessness that will not lift, please speak with your physician or a licensed mental health professional. The National Institute of Mental Health has a clear guide on caring for your mental health and when to reach out. If you ever have thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 in the US right away.

Pause for reflection

Place a hand over your heart. Breathe slowly and feel the steady rhythm beneath your palm.

Ask yourself gently: Where in my life am I still making myself smaller than I am? What would one small, brave step look like this week, even if I take it with shaking hands?

"For God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control" (2 Timothy 1:7, ESV).

You are not too much. You are not too little. You are exactly the size you were made to be.

FAQ

Can you build confidence as an adult?

Yes. Confidence grows through repeated experiences of being safe while being seen. That can happen at any age.

What is the difference between confidence and arrogance?

Arrogance needs to prove something. Confidence does not. A confident person can listen, admit mistakes, and still stand steady.

Why do I feel confident at work but not in relationships?

Different settings can trigger different old patterns. Many people feel safe in structured roles and exposed in close relationships, where early lessons about love were learned.
