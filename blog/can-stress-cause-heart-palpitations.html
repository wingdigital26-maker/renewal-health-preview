Can Stress Cause Heart Palpitations? Why Your Heart Races or Flutters

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 20, 2026

Yes, stress can cause heart palpitations, the feeling that your heart is pounding, racing, fluttering, or skipping a beat. Stress hormones speed the heart up as part of the body's alarm response. Because palpitations can also come from heart and thyroid conditions, please have new or frequent ones checked by your physician.

Why does my heart race when I am stressed?

When your nervous system senses danger, it releases adrenaline and cortisol to get you ready to act. The heart beats faster and harder so blood can reach your muscles quickly. That is a wise design for running from a real threat. The trouble is that the body responds the same way to an overflowing inbox, a hard conversation, or a worry that circles at night.

Here is what I see in the people I sit with. Many of them have lived in what I call a sympathetic lock, where the alarm switch stays partly on for months or years. Their hearts are not misbehaving. They are faithfully responding to a body that has not yet been told it is safe. I call symptoms messengers rather than enemies, and a racing heart is often saying, "We have been on guard a very long time."

Can anxiety cause heart palpitations at night?

It can. Many people notice their heartbeat most when they lie down, because the room is quiet and there is nothing else to pull their attention away. Lying on the left side can also make the beat feel louder. If your mind starts replaying the day as your head hits the pillow, your body may answer with a surge of alarm. My post on why you cannot fall asleep at night looks at that wired, wide-awake feeling.

Other everyday things can add to palpitations, such as caffeine, nicotine, alcohol, dehydration, skipped meals, poor sleep, and some medications and supplements. The MedlinePlus page on heart palpitations gives a clear overview of common causes.

How do I know if palpitations are from stress?

Only a physician can tell you for sure, and that is an important first step. Palpitations are more likely to be connected to stress when:

  • They show up during or right after stressful moments.
  • They come with other stress signals, like shallow breathing or a tight chest.
  • They ease as you rest, breathe slowly, or feel safe again.
  • Your physician has checked your heart and found nothing concerning.

How can I calm heart palpitations from stress?

Think of these as small signals of safety, offered alongside the care your physician recommends.

  1. Lengthen the exhale. Breathe in gently through the nose and let the out-breath be longer than the in-breath. The exhale is the body's built-in brake.
  2. Put your feet on the ground. Feel the floor beneath you and name a few things you can see. This helps the brain notice the present.
  3. Place a hand over your heart. Warm, steady pressure can feel comforting and grounding.
  4. Hum softly. A low hum gently engages the nerves that help slow the body down. My post on vagal tone exercises explains more.
  5. Notice your stimulants. Pay attention to coffee, energy drinks, and late-day caffeine, and talk with your physician about any medications.
  6. Build in pauses. Small rests through the day keep the alarm from building to a peak.

In my book, Nothing Missing, Nothing Broken, I write about how the body, soul, and spirit learn to come out of survival mode together.

When are heart palpitations an emergency?

Please call emergency services right away if palpitations come with chest pain or pressure, fainting or nearly fainting, severe shortness of breath, confusion, or pain spreading to the arm, jaw, or back. See your physician promptly if palpitations are new, happen often, last more than a few minutes, or if you have a history of heart disease. Natural-first support works best alongside your medical care, never instead of it.

Pause for Reflection

Place one hand over your heart. Feel it beating for you, steady and faithful, even now.

Ask gently: What has my heart been bracing against? Where in my life have I not yet felt safe enough to rest?

"Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid" (John 14:27). Your heart has been working so hard to protect you. It is allowed to be tended, too.

FAQ

How long do stress palpitations last?

Many people find they last seconds to a few minutes and fade as the stress settles. Palpitations that last longer, keep coming back, or come with other symptoms need a physician's evaluation.

Can dehydration make palpitations worse?

Yes, dehydration can make the heart work harder, and skipped meals can add to it. Steady water and nourishing meals are simple ways to support a calmer body.

Should I avoid caffeine if I have palpitations?

Caffeine is a common trigger for many people. It is worth noticing whether your palpitations follow coffee or energy drinks, and talking with your physician about what is right for you.
