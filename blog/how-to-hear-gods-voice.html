How Do You Hear God's Voice When Fear Is So Loud?

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · September 29, 2026

You hear God's voice most clearly when your body is quiet enough to listen. Fear is loud, urgent, and pushes you to act now. God's voice is steady, patient, and leaves peace behind it. Learning to hear it starts with slowing your body down, then noticing which voice brings rest and which one brings panic.

Why is it so hard to hear God's voice?

For many of us, the problem is not that God has gone quiet. It is that everything else is so loud.

When your nervous system is braced, your body is scanning for danger. In that state, the loudest voice in the room always wins, and fear is very good at being loud. It speaks in emergencies: "Do something. Fix it. You are running out of time."

In my book, Nothing Missing, Nothing Broken, I write about living from the Spirit, where the Holy Spirit becomes the primary authority in your life instead of the frantic dictates of fear. That shift is spiritual, and it is also physical. A body stuck in fight or flight has a hard time hearing anything gentle.

How do you tell God's voice from your own fear?

Here is the simplest test I know. Pay attention to what each voice leaves behind.

Fear's voice tends to:

  • Rush you. Everything has to be decided right now.
  • Shame you. It speaks about what is wrong with you.
  • Isolate you. It tells you to handle it alone and tell no one.
  • Tighten your body. Your jaw sets, your breath climbs into your chest.

God's voice tends to:

  • Wait. It is patient enough to be repeated.
  • Invite. It calls you toward something rather than attacking who you are.
  • Connect. It often nudges you toward wise people, not away from them.
  • Settle your body. Even hard truth arrives with a sense of being held.

When Elijah was exhausted and afraid, God was not in the wind, the earthquake, or the fire, but in "a still small voice" (1 Kings 19:12, KJV). That word "still" matters. Stillness is the language the body understands as safety.

What does your body have to do with hearing God?

More than most of us were taught.

I describe people as triune: Spirit, Soul, and Body. The Spirit leads, the Soul interprets, and the Body is the most honest witness you have. When the Body is bracing, the Soul tends to tell fear stories, and the Spirit's quieter voice gets drowned out.

This is why I often encourage people to stabilize the body first. My post on the body, soul, and spirit healing order walks through why. It is not that faith comes last. It is that a calmer body gives your faith room to breathe.

If you tend to freeze on every choice, you may also find help in my post on why you second-guess every decision, which looks at how a braced body wears down self-trust.

How do you practice listening for God's voice?

Gently, and in small daily rhythms.

1. Slow your breath first. Before you pray, take three slow breaths with a longer exhale than inhale. You are telling your body that it is safe enough to listen.

2. Ask one simple question. Instead of a long list, try: "What is Yours to carry, and what is mine?" Then wait. Silence is not failure.

3. Write down what comes. A short journal line, a verse that surfaces, a person who comes to mind. Over weeks, patterns become easier to see.

4. Test it against peace and Scripture. A nudge that is kind, patient, and consistent with Scripture is worth holding. A nudge that is frantic and shaming is worth setting down.

5. Check it with trusted people. Connection is the soil of healing. A wise friend or pastor can help you discern what you cannot see alone.

What if I still do not hear anything?

That is a common experience, and it does not mean you are doing it wrong or that you are forgotten.

Sometimes God speaks through quiet provision, a conversation, a sense of peace about a next small step. Sometimes the season is simply one of trust. Keep showing up. Keep your body soft. Listening is a practice, not a performance.

When should you get extra support?

If you are hearing voices that others do not hear, or inner messages that tell you to harm yourself or someone else, please talk with your physician or a licensed mental health professional right away. That is a health concern that deserves real care, and seeking it is an act of faith, not a lack of it. If you are in crisis in the US, you can call or text 988. The National Institute of Mental Health lists more places to find help.

Pause for Reflection

Place a hand on your heart and let your exhale lengthen.

Ask yourself gently: Which voice have I been obeying lately, the one that rushes me or the one that settles me? What might I hear if I gave my body five quiet minutes today?

You are not forgotten. You are being spoken to. You are learning to listen.

FAQ

What does God's voice sound like?

For most people it is not audible. It often comes as a quiet sense of direction, a verse that surfaces, or peace about a next step.

How do I know if it is God or my own thoughts?

Look at the fruit. God's voice tends to bring peace, patience, and clarity. Fear tends to bring urgency, shame, and isolation.

Can anxiety make it harder to hear God?

Yes. A braced nervous system narrows your attention toward threat, which makes quiet guidance harder to notice. Calming the body often helps.

Where can I start if I want support?

Start with three slow breaths and one honest question today. If you would like support, you can start a conversation through the contact page, or read about my emotional overwhelm and nervous system support.
