What Is a Highly Sensitive Person? Signs and Support

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · November 19, 2026

A highly sensitive person is someone with a deeper than usual sensitivity to the physical, emotional and social world around them. Cleveland Clinic describes it as a personality trait, not a mental health disorder. Highly sensitive people often feel things deeply, notice subtle details, and become overwhelmed more quickly by noise, crowds or conflict.

What does it mean to be a highly sensitive person?

"Stop being so sensitive." If you heard that growing up, you may have learned to treat your sensitivity as a flaw. It is not one.

Cleveland Clinic explains that psychologists began using the term in the 1990s to describe people with a heightened awareness of what is happening around them. It is also called sensory processing sensitivity. There is no official test for it and no official diagnosis, because it is a trait, a way of being made, and not an illness.

Your system simply takes in more. That can be a gift and a weight in the same afternoon.

What are the signs of a highly sensitive person?

Cleveland Clinic lists traits that tend to be common, including:

  • Avoiding violent shows or movies
  • Finding beauty in almost anything, in art or in nature
  • Feeling overwhelmed by noise, bright lights or uncomfortable clothes
  • Feeling anxious
  • Needing downtime
  • Having a rich inner life

You might also pick up on other people's moods before they say a word, or need longer to recover after a busy day. If that sounds familiar, my post on why you get overstimulated so easily looks at what is happening in your body in those moments.

Is being highly sensitive the same as being an introvert?

No, although they can look alike. Cleveland Clinic explains that an introvert may be worn out by the social side of a gathering, like small talk or a large group. A highly sensitive person may also be affected by the bright lights and loud music in the room. One is about people. The other is about the whole environment.

Is high sensitivity the same as ADHD or autism?

No. Cleveland Clinic is clear that high sensitivity is often mistaken for ADHD or autism, but it is not the same thing and is not a sign of either. It can, however, exist alongside them. A person can be highly sensitive and also have ADHD, for example.

If you are trying to understand what is going on for you or your child, a doctor or qualified mental health professional is the right place to begin. For families who already know they are walking a neurodivergent path, my neurodivergent support page explains how I come alongside that care with a calmer body and gentler rhythms.

Why are some people more sensitive than others?

It is thought to be partly inherited. Cleveland Clinic adds that environment and childhood experiences can play a part, and that people who lived through hard things early on may become more watchful, more alert to what could go wrong.

I find this deeply tender. Some sensitivity is simply how you were knit together. Some of it is a nervous system that learned to scan the room because, once, it needed to. Both deserve kindness. Neither is a defect.

How can a highly sensitive person care for themselves?

You do not need to toughen up. You need to tend the system you have. Cleveland Clinic suggests several strategies, and they are wonderfully ordinary:

  • Take time for yourself. Downtime is not indulgence. It is how your system clears what it took in.
  • Set boundaries. It is fine to step back from situations that flood you. My post on how to set boundaries without guilt can help with the guilt that follows.
  • Keep a few close relationships. Depth suits you better than breadth, and it is fine to limit time with people who drain you.
  • Practice gratitude. Naming a few things you are thankful for, morning and night, gives a sensitive mind somewhere gentle to rest.
  • Consider talk therapy. If your sensitivity is making daily life hard, a counselor can help you build coping skills.
A gentle practice: after any busy stretch, give yourself ten quiet minutes before the next thing. Dim the lights. Put your phone in another room. Let your senses come back down the way a snow globe settles when you finally set it on the table.

When is sensitivity a sign you need more support?

Sensitivity by itself is not a problem to solve. But if you feel anxious most days, if overwhelm is keeping you from work, relationships or rest, or if sadness is not lifting, please talk with your doctor or a mental health professional. You can read more in the Cleveland Clinic article on highly sensitive people.

FAQ

Is being a highly sensitive person a disorder?

No. Cleveland Clinic describes it as a personality trait, not a mental health disorder. There is no official diagnosis or test for it.

Can you be highly sensitive and an extrovert?

Yes. Sensitivity is about how deeply you take in your surroundings, not about how much you enjoy people. Some highly sensitive people are very social and simply need more recovery time afterward.

Do highly sensitive people get overwhelmed more easily?

Often, yes. Taking in more sound, light and emotion means the system fills up faster. Regular downtime and clear boundaries can make a real difference.
