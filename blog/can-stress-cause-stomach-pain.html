Can Stress Cause Stomach Pain? Why Your Gut Feels What You Carry

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 27, 2026

Yes, stress can cause stomach pain. Your gut and brain are in constant conversation, so when your body senses pressure, digestion slows, the belly muscles tighten, and the gut becomes more sensitive to normal sensations. The result can be knots, cramps, or an ache. Pain that is severe, new, or lasting needs your physician's care.

Why does my stomach hurt when I am stressed?

Your gut is one of the most honest witnesses you have. It is lined with a vast network of nerves, sometimes called the second brain, and it talks with your brain all day long through the vagus nerve and the chemistry of stress.

When your nervous system senses a threat, it shifts you out of rest-and-digest and into protection. Blood moves toward your arms and legs. Digestion is placed on hold, because in a true emergency, breakfast can wait. The muscles of the belly brace, the way they would if you expected a blow.

For a short moment, that is wisdom. Trouble comes when the alarm stays on. I call this the sympathetic lock: the body keeps bracing long after the danger has passed. A belly that is braced for weeks can feel tight, crampy, and sore, and it may also become more aware of ordinary gas and movement that a calm gut would never notice. I explain more of this two-way traffic in my post on the gut-brain connection.

What does stress stomach pain feel like?

People describe it in many ways. Some feel a tight knot just under the ribs. Some feel cramping low in the belly. Others feel a dull ache that comes and goes with the day, or a churning that shows up before a meeting, a phone call, or a family visit.

Many people notice a pattern. The ache arrives on Sunday evening, or the morning of a hard task, and eases on a gentle day. If you know that Sunday feeling, you may find words for it in my post on why you get anxious on Sunday nights.

A woman once told me, almost apologizing, that her stomach "always overreacts." I told her what I tell everyone: symptoms are messengers, not enemies. Her gut was not overreacting. It was reporting, faithfully, how much she had been holding.

Can anxiety cause stomach pain every day?

It can. When stress is constant, the gut may never get a full turn in rest-and-digest. Meals get rushed or skipped. Sleep runs short. Coffee stands in for breakfast. Each of these can add to the discomfort, and over time the belly can start to feel tender most days.

The Harvard Health article on the gut-brain connection describes how a troubled brain can send signals to the gut, and a troubled gut can send signals back. That loop is real. It is also not the whole story, so daily pain still deserves a conversation with your doctor, who can look for other causes.

What helps stomach pain from stress?

Think of these as small signals of safety, offered alongside the care your physician recommends.

  1. Breathe low and slow. Rest a hand on your belly. Breathe in for a count of four and let the out-breath last for six. Let your belly rise under your hand. A long exhale is one of the clearest ways to tell your body it can soften.
  2. Eat sitting down. Your gut digests best when your body believes it is safe. Sit, slow down, and take a few breaths before the first bite.
  3. Use gentle warmth. A warm drink or a warm cloth over the belly can feel like a quiet message to the muscles that they can let go.
  4. Notice the timing. Keep a few notes on when the ache comes. Patterns are information, not judgment, and they are helpful to bring to your doctor.
  5. Give your body true rest. I write about why the gut cannot heal while it is bracing in rest and digest.

In my gut health support, I look at nourishment, rhythm, the nervous system, and the stories the body is holding, because a body heals under safety, not pressure.

When should I see a doctor about stomach pain?

Please see your physician if your stomach pain is new, keeps coming back, wakes you at night, or comes with weight loss you cannot explain, a change in bowel habits, trouble swallowing, or ongoing vomiting.

Seek emergency care right away for sudden, severe belly pain, pain with a high fever, vomiting blood, black or bloody stools, a hard and tender belly, pain during pregnancy, or pain that spreads to the chest, neck, or shoulder. The MedlinePlus guide to abdominal pain is a clear place to learn more.

Pause for Reflection

Rest one hand over your belly and one over your heart. Breathe slowly and feel your hands rise and fall.

Ask gently: What have I been bracing against? What would it feel like to let my belly soften, even a little, right now?

"Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee" (Psalm 55:22). Your belly was never meant to carry what your heart can set down.

FAQ

Can stress cause stomach pain without other symptoms?

Yes. Stress can bring cramping or an ache on its own, without nausea or changes in digestion. Because many conditions can cause stomach pain, it is still wise to let your physician check pain that keeps returning.

How long does stress stomach pain last?

It often eases as the stressful moment passes and the body settles. Pain that lasts for days, keeps coming back, or grows worse needs a medical evaluation.

Can calming my nervous system replace seeing a doctor for stomach pain?

No. Gentle nervous system support can be offered alongside medical care, never instead of it. Your doctor should always rule out other causes first.
