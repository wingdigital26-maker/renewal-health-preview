Why Do I Get Sick So Often When I Am Stressed?

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · September 22, 2026

Sustained stress changes immune function. Cortisol dampens parts of the immune response over time, which is why long pressure is associated with more frequent infection and slower recovery. Falling ill when the pressure finally lifts is a recognised pattern too. The immune system is not failing you. It has been running on a setting it was never meant to hold.

How does stress actually affect the immune system?

Through chemistry, not character.

Your immune system is not a single organ. It is distributed: the gut, the lymph nodes, the spleen, the bone marrow, the mucosal linings of the nose and throat, all in constant conversation with the nervous and endocrine systems. When the body reads threat, it prioritises. Blood goes to muscle, digestion slows, and the expensive work of immune surveillance is set aside for getting through the next ten minutes.

Over a short burst that is elegant design. Over years it is costly. Cortisol has a powerful anti inflammatory effect, and prolonged exposure is associated with a blunted immune response, one reason chronic stress is linked with more frequent infection and slower healing. The National Institute of Mental Health's overview of chronic stress sets out what sustained stress does across the body.

None of this means stress causes any particular illness. It means the terrain changes.

Why do I get sick as soon as I relax?

Because the bracing was holding the line, and then the line came down.

People describe this almost identically. The exam finishes, the project ships, the holiday starts, and within two days there is a sore throat. It is common enough to have a folk name, the let down effect, and it makes sense once you stop thinking of stress as a feeling and start treating it as a physiological state.

Say plainly what this is not. It is not proof that you cannot handle rest, and certainly not a reason to keep bracing. It is a sign the body has carried a load without an off ramp for a long time.

What is the connection between the gut and immunity?

A large share of the body's immune tissue sits in the gut, which is why digestion and immunity move together.

If you have been under long pressure, digestion has been running on a low setting: blood redirected away, enzymes suppressed, motility slowed. The gut lining and its resident microbes are part of your immune defence, not separate from it, so a digestive system sidelined for years is an immune system working with less support. That is why gut health support and inflammation support are one conversation in my practice, not two appointments.

What does sustained stress have to do with autoimmune conditions?

This is where care matters more than enthusiasm.

Autoimmune conditions involve the immune system losing part of its ability to tell the body's own tissue from a threat. Stress is one factor studied among many, alongside genetics, infection and environment, and the research is still developing. Nobody can responsibly tell you stress caused your condition, and nobody should tell you managing stress will resolve it.

What I will say is that the pattern interests me. The immune system's difficulty in telling self from threat echoes something I see every week, and it is the thread running through my book Nothing Missing, Nothing Broken: the difficulty of telling an inherited wound from your own identity. That is a reflection, not a mechanism.

If you have a diagnosed autoimmune condition, your specialist leads. Work like mine sits alongside that care, never instead of it.

What actually helps immune resilience?

Nothing you have not heard, which is the point.

  • Sleep. The single most consequential lever, and the least glamorous.
  • Digestion. Fibre, fermented foods if they agree with you, and meals eaten sitting down.
  • Real recovery, not collapse. Scrolling on the sofa sends the body a different signal than a walk, a slow breath or a conversation.
  • Movement and breath. Lymph has no pump of its own. It moves when you do.
  • Fewer simultaneous loads. Sometimes the honest intervention is subtraction.

If rest itself feels unsafe, start upstream of the immune conversation. Support for emotional overwhelm and the nervous system is where that work belongs, and why you feel wired but tired describes the state I mean.

When should you see a doctor about getting sick often?

Have frequent infection properly assessed rather than filed under stress.

Book an appointment if infections are unusually frequent, keep returning, or are severe or slow to clear, and if you have unexplained fevers, night sweats, weight loss, swollen glands that do not settle, or persistent fatigue. Ask about a full blood count, iron, vitamin D, thyroid function, blood glucose, and a review of any medication affecting immune function. There are treatable causes unrelated to stress, worth ruling out first.

Pause for reflection

Sit back. Let your shoulders drop a little further than feels necessary.

Then ask gently: when did I last let my body finish something rather than move straight into the next demand? And what would it take to give it one true off ramp this week?

"Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28). Rest is offered, not earned.

Your body is not betraying you. It has been standing guard for a very long time.

FAQ

Can stress really make you more likely to catch a cold?

Research links sustained psychological stress with greater susceptibility to respiratory infection and slower recovery. That association is seen across groups of people and does not predict what happens to any one person.

Why do I always get ill on the first day of a holiday?

The shift out of a prolonged stress state changes immune signalling, and the timing is common enough to be widely recognised. It reflects how long the pressure ran, not whether you deserved the break.

Does taking supplements strengthen immunity?

Please do not self prescribe. Some nutrients matter if you are genuinely low in them, which is what testing is for, and several interact with prescription medication. Ask your physician first.

Where should I start?

Start with sleep and one real off ramp this week. If frequent illness has become a pattern you want looked at as a whole, you can start a conversation through the contact page.
