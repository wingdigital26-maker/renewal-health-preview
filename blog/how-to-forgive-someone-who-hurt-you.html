How Do You Forgive Someone Who Hurt You?

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 1, 2026

To forgive someone who hurt you, start by naming the hurt honestly, letting your body feel safe again, and then choosing, often many times, to release your claim on repayment. Forgiveness is not excusing the harm, forgetting it, or letting the person back in. It is setting down a weight you were never meant to carry.

What does it really mean to forgive someone?

Many of us were taught that forgiving means pretending it did not hurt, or acting as if nothing happened. That is not forgiveness. That is suppression, and the body keeps the receipt.

The Mayo Clinic describes forgiveness as a combination of acceptance and an intentional decision to let go of resentment, and notes that it does not mean forgetting or excusing what happened, and does not necessarily mean making up with the person.

I would put it this way: forgiveness is not a verdict that the harm was acceptable. It is a decision that the harm will no longer be the thing that governs you.

Why is it so hard to forgive someone who hurt you?

Because your body remembers. When someone wounds you, especially someone you trusted, your nervous system files them under danger. Every memory, every replay, every time their name comes up, your body braces as if the hurt is happening again.

So when you tell yourself to "just forgive," you are often asking a guarded body to drop its guard before it feels safe. That rarely works. You cannot think your way out of a nervous system that still believes it is under threat.

This is why forgiveness can feel like trying to open a door your body is holding shut. The door is not stubbornness. It is protection.

Does forgiving mean I have to reconcile or trust them again?

No. Forgiveness and reconciliation are two different things.

  • Forgiveness happens inside you. It is between you and God, and it does not require the other person's apology, participation, or even awareness.
  • Reconciliation happens between two people. It requires safety, honesty and changed behavior, and sometimes it is not wise or possible.
  • Trust is rebuilt slowly, through action over time. It is earned, not owed.

You can have a heart full of compassion and a front door that is firmly locked. If setting limits stirs up guilt, my post on how to set boundaries without guilt walks through why both can be true at once.

If you are in a relationship where you are being harmed or do not feel safe, forgiveness never means staying. Please talk with someone you trust, or call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 in the US.

How do you start forgiving when you are still angry?

Anger is not the opposite of forgiveness. Often it is the doorway into it. Anger says, "That mattered. I mattered." Let it be true before you try to move past it.

A gentle path many people find helpful:

  1. Name the hurt plainly. Write down what happened and what it cost you, without softening it.
  2. Let your body settle first. Slow breathing, a walk, or a hand on your chest helps your nervous system come down from high alert before you try to process the memory.
  3. Grieve what you lost. Forgiveness often holds grief underneath it. My post on why grief sits in your chest explores how that grief can live in the body.
  4. Release the debt, not the truth. You are not saying it was fine. You are saying, "You no longer owe me, and I no longer have to collect."
  5. Expect to return. Forgiveness is rarely one moment. The feeling may come back, and you can choose again from a steadier place.

What does unforgiveness do to your body?

Holding on to resentment keeps your stress response running in the background. I have noticed this in my own life: whenever I feel completely overwhelmed, it is usually a signal that I have picked up something that does not belong to me. I have to stop and ask, "What did I pick up when I was not looking? And whose weight is this, really?"

Resentment is one of those weights. It keeps the body braced, breath shallow and sleep light. Laying it down is not only a spiritual act. It is a physical one. When the alarm finally quiets, your body can turn its energy back toward rest, digestion and repair. This is a big part of the emotional overwhelm and nervous system support I offer: helping the body feel safe enough to let go.

Pause for Reflection

Place a hand on your heart and let your exhale grow a little longer than your inhale.

Ask yourself gently: What am I still carrying from this hurt? What would it feel like in my body to set down even a small part of it today?

"Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you" (Colossians 3:13, NIV). You are not asked to carry this alone, and you are not asked to do it all at once.

You are not weak for still hurting. You are brave for being willing to release it.

FAQ

Can you forgive someone without telling them?

Yes. Forgiveness is an inner release. You do not need a conversation, an apology, or contact with the person to forgive them.

How long does it take to forgive someone?

There is no set timeline. Deeper wounds often take longer, and forgiveness may happen in layers over months or years.

Is it okay if I never forget what happened?

Yes. Forgiveness does not erase memory. The goal is that the memory no longer controls your body or your choices.
