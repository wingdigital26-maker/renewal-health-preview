Can Stress Cause Tinnitus? Ringing Ears in Loud Seasons

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 18, 2026

Many people notice that ringing in the ears, called tinnitus, feels louder during stressful or anxious seasons. Stress does not usually start tinnitus on its own, but a nervous system on alert tends to turn up attention to inner sounds and make them harder to tune out. Any new or sudden ear change deserves a prompt visit to your physician.

Can anxiety make tinnitus worse?

Yes, for many people the two travel together. Tinnitus is the experience of hearing a sound, such as ringing, buzzing, humming, or hissing, that is not coming from the world around you. The National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders describes it as very common and notes that it often goes along with hearing changes.

Here is what I see in the people I sit with. When the body is braced, the brain becomes a watchful security guard. It scans for anything unusual, and an inner sound is exactly the kind of thing it locks onto. The more you notice the ringing, the more anxious you feel, and the more anxious you feel, the louder the ringing seems. It is a loop, not a flaw in you.

I often call symptoms messengers rather than enemies. A ringing that swells in a hard week may be your body saying, "We have been on high alert for a long time."

Why do my ears ring more when I am stressed?

Stress rarely travels alone. A few patterns tend to show up together:

  • Held tension. A clenched jaw and tight neck and shoulders are common in stressful seasons, and many people notice their ears feel noisier when those muscles are tight. My post on whether stress can cause headaches looks at that same tension pattern.
  • Short sleep. Tired brains are less able to filter out background sounds.
  • Quiet rooms. At night, when the house goes still, inner sounds stand out.
  • More caffeine and screens. Both keep an already alert system revved.
  • Body watching. Anxiety turns attention inward, and every sensation feels louder.

Some people also notice dizziness or a sense of fullness in the ears when they are stressed. My post on whether stress can cause dizziness explores that unsteady feeling and when it needs a closer look.

How can I calm tinnitus from stress?

Think of these as small signals of safety, offered alongside the care your physician or hearing specialist recommends.

  1. Slow the exhale. Breathe in gently through the nose and let the out-breath be longer than the in-breath. A slower rhythm tells the body the emergency has passed.
  2. Add soft sound. A fan, gentle music, or nature sounds at a low volume can make the ringing less noticeable in a quiet room.
  3. Soften the jaw and neck. Let the tongue rest, unclench the teeth, and slowly roll the shoulders a few times a day.
  4. Protect your ears. Turn headphone volume down and use ear protection around loud tools, concerts, or machinery.
  5. Shift your attention kindly. When you catch yourself listening for the sound, name one thing you can see, one you can feel, and one you can hear outside you.
  6. Protect your sleep. A steady bedtime and a wind-down hour without screens is one of the kindest things you can give a watchful brain.

When should I see a doctor about ringing in my ears?

Please see a physician right away if you have sudden hearing loss in one or both ears, since the NIDCD notes that sudden hearing loss is a medical emergency where prompt care matters. See a physician promptly if tinnitus is only in one ear, beats in time with your heartbeat, comes with dizziness, ear pain, or drainage, follows a head injury, or began after starting a new medication.

Please also reach out if the ringing is affecting your sleep, mood, or daily life. Your physician or an audiologist can check your hearing and look for causes such as earwax, ear conditions, or medication effects. Natural-first support works best alongside your medical care, never instead of it.

Pause for Reflection

Place one hand over your heart and let your exhale be slow. Let your jaw drop just a little.

Ask gently: What has my body been listening for lately? Where am I bracing for the next thing? What would it feel like to let the world around me be the loudest sound for one minute?

When Elijah was worn out and afraid, God was not in the wind, the earthquake, or the fire, but in "a still small voice" (1 Kings 19:12). Even in a noisy season, there is a quiet place within you where you are held.

FAQ

Can stress tinnitus go away?

Many people notice their tinnitus feels quieter as stress, sleep, and tension improve, while for others it stays but becomes less bothersome. A hearing evaluation is the best place to understand your own situation.

Why is my tinnitus louder at night?

At night the background noise of the day falls away, so inner sounds stand out. Tiredness and the day's stress can also catch up with the body in the evening. Soft background sound often helps.
