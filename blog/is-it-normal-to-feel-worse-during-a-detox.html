Is It Normal to Feel Worse When You Start a Detox?

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · September 22, 2026

Feeling rough in the first days of a cleanse is common, but it is not evidence that the protocol is working. Most of it comes from what was removed rather than what was released: caffeine, sugar, calories, sleep. A well paced approach opens the body's exit routes first, and it should not require you to feel unwell.

Why do some people feel worse when they start a detox?

Usually for reasons less mysterious than the marketing suggests.

Most cleanse programs change several things at once. Caffeine disappears, and withdrawal produces headache, fatigue and low mood within a day. Sugar drops sharply. Calories drop, which affects blood sugar, concentration and temper. Sleep shifts. Any one explains a bad Tuesday on its own, and most protocols do all four together.

Then there is the part that is real physiology. When you increase fluids, fibre, movement and bitter foods, you ask the liver and the lymph to pick up their pace. If the routes out are sluggish, if you are constipated, dehydrated, barely moving or breathing shallowly, material gets mobilised faster than it gets eliminated. That is not a mystical event. It is a traffic problem, and it feels like a headache.

Is a healing crisis a real thing?

Here is the honest answer. "Healing crisis" is not an established medical concept, and it should not be used to explain away symptoms.

The phrase is used widely in natural health, and I understand why. But a term that recasts every bad outcome as secretly good is not a diagnostic tool. It is a way of not looking.

Feeling unwell is information. Sometimes it is that you removed your coffee. Sometimes you are dehydrated. Sometimes something else is going on that has nothing to do with the cleanse. Your body is the most honest witness you have, and it deserves to be read rather than overruled.

Which symptoms mean slow down, and which mean stop?

Slow down for the mild and familiar: a dull headache, tiredness, irritability, slower digestion, mild nausea, a night of poorer sleep. These usually ease with more water, a proper meal, a walk, and less intensity.

Stop and get assessed for anything sharp, severe or unfamiliar. Vomiting that will not settle, fainting, chest pain, a racing heart, severe abdominal pain, fever, a spreading rash, confusion, very dark urine or passing little urine, a severe or worsening headache, or any symptom that frightens you. Call your physician, and call emergency services if it is sudden or severe. None of that is a detox reaction to push through.

Stop too if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, live with kidney or liver disease, diabetes, or a history of disordered eating, or take prescription medication. Cleanses interact with all of those, and that conversation belongs with your doctor first. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health's summary of detoxes and cleanses is a clear, sober place to read more.

How do you open the exit ramps first?

This is the part most programs skip, and the part that changes the experience.

Think of the body's elimination routes as highway exit ramps: the bowel, the kidneys, the liver and bile, the lymph, the skin and the breath. Close the ramps, send more traffic onto the road, and everything backs up. Order matters more than ingredients.

  • Bowel first. Daily, comfortable elimination is the baseline. Fibre, water and movement.
  • Hydration next. The kidneys cannot filter what you have not given them.
  • Then movement and breath. The lymphatic system has no pump of its own. Walking and slow, low breathing do that work.
  • Then, and only then, anything that mobilises. Bitters, binders, herbs, fasting windows.

I have written about that sequence in more detail in how the body's drainage pathways work, and about the pacing itself in what a gentle detox actually looks like. The principle underneath both is simple. The body releases under safety, not under pressure.

Does feeling nothing mean it is not working?

No. Quiet is not failure.

We have absorbed a strange idea that intensity equals effectiveness, that if a thing does not hurt it is not working. The changes I see last are undramatic: sleep deepens, digestion settles, the afternoon stops collapsing. Nobody posts about that. It is still the thing you wanted.

If you have been pushing hard at your health for years and landing in the same place, the free start here guide is a quieter entry point than another protocol.

When should you work with a practitioner instead?

When the picture is bigger than a week of green juice.

If several cleanses have landed you in the same place each time, that pattern is worth understanding rather than repeating. Detox support in my practice is about restoring the pathways, and the nervous system underneath them, at a pace your body agrees to. Slower than a fourteen day reset, and considerably more useful.

Pause for reflection

Put a hand on your abdomen and let your breath drop lower than usual. No effort. Just lower.

Then ask gently: where did I learn that improvement has to hurt? And what would change if my body were not being stubborn, only overloaded?

"He restores my soul" (Psalm 23:3). Restores. Not overhauls.

You are not failing the protocol. The protocol may simply be asking more of you than your exits can carry today.

FAQ

How long do detox symptoms usually last?

Mild effects from removing caffeine and sugar typically settle within a few days. Feeling unwell for longer is a signal to stop and reassess with your physician, not a phase to endure.

Are detox symptoms proof that toxins are leaving?

No. There is no reliable way to attribute a headache or fatigue to toxin release, and withdrawal, low blood sugar, dehydration and poor sleep produce the same symptoms. Discomfort is not evidence.

Should I drink more water if I feel unwell during a cleanse?

Water helps, and it is a sensible first move alongside a real meal and a walk. It is no substitute for medical assessment if symptoms are severe, unusual or worsening.

Where should I start?

Start with the unglamorous foundations for a week: daily elimination, water, a short walk, low breathing. If you would like support shaped around your own history rather than a generic protocol, you can start a conversation through the contact page.
