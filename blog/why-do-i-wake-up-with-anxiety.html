Why Do I Wake Up With Anxiety Every Morning?

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 5, 2026

You may wake up with anxiety because your body naturally releases more cortisol in the early morning, and a nervous system that has been on guard for a long time can read that rise as danger. Poor sleep, a racing mind, and blood sugar dips can add to it. Your body is not broken. It is bracing out of habit.

Is it normal to wake up with anxiety?

Many people know this feeling well. Your eyes open, and before you have had a single thought, your chest is tight, your stomach is fluttering, or your mind is already running through the day. Nothing has happened yet, and still your body feels like something is wrong.

Morning anxiety is common, and it is not a sign of weakness. It is a pattern your body has learned, and patterns that are learned can be gently unlearned. The question I invite you to ask is not "What is wrong with me?" but "What is my body trying to protect me from?"

Why is anxiety worse in the morning?

Part of the answer is a normal rhythm. Cortisol, one of your main stress hormones, is meant to rise in the early morning to help you wake up and get moving. The Cleveland Clinic explains cortisol as a hormone that helps regulate your stress response, energy, and sleep-wake cycle.

In a settled body, that morning rise feels like readiness. In a body that has been braced for months, the same rise can feel like alarm. I call this state "sympathetic lock," where the body treats being on guard as its default. When you wake, your system does not start from calm. It starts from the last place it remembers: ready for trouble.

Low blood sugar after a long night, and worries carried to bed, can add to it.

Can stress and poor sleep cause morning anxiety?

Yes, and they often feed each other. When stress is high, sleep becomes lighter and more broken. The body never fully lets down its guard, so you wake unrefreshed and already tense. Then that tension makes the next night harder.

If you sleep a full night and still wake exhausted, my post on waking up tired after a full night of sleep explores why. And if you feel exhausted all day but switched on at night, that pattern is something I describe as being "wired but tired."

How do you calm anxiety when you first wake up?

You do not have to fix the whole day before your feet touch the floor. A few gentle places to begin:

  1. Stay still for one minute. Before reaching for your phone, place a hand on your chest and notice your breath. Let your body know there is no emergency.
  2. Lengthen the exhale. Breathe in for four and out for six, five or six times. A longer out-breath signals safety to your nervous system.
  3. Name three things you can sense. The weight of the blanket, the light at the window, a sound in the house. This brings you into the present moment, where the day has not gone wrong yet.
  4. Get morning light. Open the curtains or step outside for a few minutes. Light helps set your body's daily rhythm.
  5. Eat a steady breakfast. Some protein early can help steady energy and mood for many people.

If your mind tends to spiral as soon as you wake, my post on how to stop overthinking offers more body-first tools.

Why does my body feel unsafe when nothing is wrong?

This is the tender center of morning anxiety. Your body is not reacting to this morning. It is reacting to everything it has carried before this morning. A nervous system that learned, often long ago, that it had to stay ready, will keep being ready until it has enough experiences of safety to believe something new.

That is why healing here is slow and gentle. Each calm morning, even a calm minute, is a small piece of evidence your body can learn from. In my book, Nothing Missing, Nothing Broken, I write about how the body softens when it finally believes it is safe. When you want personal support, my emotional overwhelm and nervous system support work looks at the whole of your life, including sleep, stress, and how your body has learned to cope.

When should you see a doctor about morning anxiety?

Please talk with your physician or a licensed mental health professional if anxiety is affecting your daily life, if it comes with panic attacks, chest pain, or a racing heart, or if you are not sleeping well for weeks at a time. Thyroid changes, blood sugar issues, and certain medications can all play a part and deserve a proper look. The National Institute of Mental Health has clear information on anxiety. If you ever have thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 in the US.

Pause for Reflection

Tomorrow morning, before anything else, place a hand over your heart. Breathe in slowly, and let the out-breath be a little longer.

Ask yourself: What is my body bracing for? What would it feel like to begin the day from safety instead of alarm?

"Let the morning bring me word of your unfailing love, for I have put my trust in you" (Psalm 143:8, NIV). The morning can become a place of welcome again, one breath at a time.

You are not starting the day behind. You are starting it held.

FAQ

Is morning anxiety caused by cortisol?

Cortisol naturally rises in the morning, and in a stressed body that rise can feel like anxiety. It is usually one part of a bigger picture that includes sleep, stress, and habits.

Does morning anxiety go away?

For many people it eases as the nervous system has more experiences of safety. If it is persistent or severe, please speak with your physician.

Can what I eat affect morning anxiety?

For some people, a long gap without food and low blood sugar can add to morning shakiness. A steady breakfast may help.
