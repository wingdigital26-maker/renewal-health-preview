Can Stress Cause Headaches? What Your Head Is Telling You

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 7, 2026

Yes, stress is one of the most common triggers for headaches, especially tension-type headaches. When you are under pressure, the muscles of your jaw, neck, and scalp tighten, your breath gets shallow, and you may skip meals or sleep. Together those can create that familiar band of pressure around the head. Any new or severe headache still needs a physician.

What does a stress headache feel like?

Most people describe it as a dull, steady pressure, like a band squeezing around the forehead, temples, or the back of the head. It often builds slowly through the day rather than striking all at once. The neck and shoulders may feel tight and tender to the touch.

MedlinePlus describes tension headaches as the most common type of headache, often linked to tight muscles in the shoulders, neck, scalp, and jaw. Stress is one of the most frequent triggers they name.

Why does stress give me a headache?

Because your head sits on top of a body that is bracing. When the nervous system senses pressure, it gets you ready to act. The jaw clenches, the shoulders lift, the eyes narrow at a screen, and the breath moves high and fast into the chest. Hold that posture for a few hours and the muscles around your skull start to complain.

There is more going on than muscles. Stress tends to pull other threads at the same time: you forget to drink water, you work through lunch, you rely on extra coffee, and you sleep lightly. Each of those can lower your headache threshold on its own. Stack them together and the headache almost writes itself.

I often say symptoms are messengers, not enemies. A stress headache is rarely random. It usually arrives with a story about the day you just lived.

Why do I get a headache after the stress is over?

Some people feel fine during a hard week and then get a headache on Saturday morning. This is sometimes called a "let-down" headache. When the pressure lifts, the body's chemistry shifts quickly, and sleeping in, a late breakfast, or less caffeine than usual can add to it.

It is a reminder that your body has been working hard the whole time, even when you felt like you were coping. Rest is not the moment you fall apart. It is the moment your body finally gets to tell the truth.

Can a tight jaw or neck cause headaches?

Very often, yes. Clenching your teeth during the day or grinding them at night keeps the jaw muscles working when they should be resting, and that tension can spread up into the temples. A neck held forward over a phone or laptop does something similar at the back of the head.

Check in right now. Are your teeth touching? Are your shoulders closer to your ears than they need to be? Simply noticing is often the first release. My post on somatic exercises to release tension has gentle ways to soften the jaw, neck, and shoulders.

How can I relieve a stress headache naturally?

Small, consistent habits can make a real difference:

  1. Unclench and breathe. Let your tongue rest on the floor of your mouth, drop your shoulders, and take a few breaths with a longer out-breath.
  2. Drink water and eat regularly. Skipped meals and dehydration are quiet headache triggers.
  3. Look away from the screen. Every so often, rest your eyes on something far away.
  4. Warmth on the neck. A warm cloth across the shoulders can help tight muscles let go.
  5. Protect your sleep and caffeine rhythm. Keeping both steady, even on weekends, can help prevent the let-down pattern.

When is a headache an emergency?

Call 911 or go to the emergency room for a sudden, severe headache that feels like the worst of your life, or a headache with confusion, trouble speaking, weakness or numbness on one side, vision loss, a stiff neck with fever, fainting, or one that follows a head injury. Please also see your physician if your headaches are new, changing, getting more frequent, waking you from sleep, or if you are relying on pain relievers several days a week.

Pause for Reflection

Rest your fingertips lightly on your temples and take a slow breath. Let your jaw soften.

Ask gently: What have I been holding my breath through? What would it feel like to let today be enough?

Your head is not your enemy. It is simply the part of you brave enough to say, "This is too much."

You are allowed to loosen your grip.

FAQ

How long does a stress headache last?

Tension-type headaches can last anywhere from a short while to most of a day. If yours are frequent or long-lasting, talk with your physician.

Can anxiety cause daily headaches?

Ongoing anxiety and stress can contribute to frequent headaches. Because many conditions can cause daily headaches, it is important to have them checked by a physician.

Is it a stress headache or a migraine?

Stress headaches usually feel like steady pressure, while migraines often throb and may come with nausea or light sensitivity. Only a physician can tell you which you are experiencing.
