When Defeat Becomes a Doorway: Growth Through Hardship

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · July 22, 2026

← Back to the blog
Defeat may serve as well as victory
To shake the soul and let the glory out.
When the great oak is straining in the wind,
The boughs drink in new beauty, and the trunk
Sends down a deeper root on the windward side.
Only the soul that knows the mighty grief
Can know the mighty rapture. Sorrows come
To stretch out spaces in the heart for joy.
Edwin Markham

Spiritual growth through hardship is not a comforting slogan; it is a pattern written into both Scripture and biology. Sometimes it is the breaking, not the winning, that frees what is most sacred inside us. The shaking is not a punishment but a loosening, an unfastening of what was too tight, too small, or too familiar.

There are lines of poetry that don't just speak, they remain. For over thirty years, Edwin Markham's words have been a steady companion to me. They have traveled through seasons of loss, rebuilding, and quiet reinvention. And somehow, they keep revealing new truths every time life shakes me awake.

He names a truth we rarely admit. We spend so much of our lives trying to tighten our grip, to secure our circumstances, and to avoid the "shaking" at all costs. But Markham suggests otherwise.

Healing often begins in the places we would never choose.

How Does the Wind Shape the Roots?

Markham's image of the great oak straining against the wind is a powerful metaphor for our own resilience. When life feels like it is bending us past our limits, we often mistake the strain for destruction. We feel the "creaking" of our internal fibers and assume we are about to snap.

But the oak isn't weakened by the storm; it is formed by it. Botanists know that trees grown in a completely windless environment, like a greenhouse, will eventually collapse under their own weight. They lack "stress wood," the specialized, dense tissue that grows only in response to the wind's pull.

The Biological Pull: The wind forces the tree to deepen its roots. It sends signals to the root system to anchor further into the earth to find stability.

The Internal Strengthening: The strain teaches the fibers where to anchor. It identifies the weak points and reinforces them.

The Resilience Factor: The pressure cultivates a resilience that cannot be manufactured in the sun.

We don't deepen when life is easy. We deepen when life demands it.

That deepening isn't about becoming harder or more cynical; it's about becoming more rooted, more aware, and more aligned with your Original Design. When the external world is in chaos, you are forced to find a foundation that is deeper than the storm.

Why Is Grief a Sacred Space-Maker?

"Only the soul that knows the mighty grief
Can know the mighty rapture."

This is perhaps the most difficult line to swallow when you are in the middle of a "mighty grief." It feels offensive to suggest that your sorrow is a prerequisite for joy. However, life has a way of teaching what poetry only hints at.

Grief stretches the heart. It carves out new chambers, new capacity, for joy, compassion, and wisdom. This is not about glorifying pain; it is about recognizing the Expansion of the Soul. Think of the heart as a vessel. If you have only ever known light, shallow happiness, the "walls" of your heart remain thin and small. But when grief enters, it stretches those walls to their breaking point.

When the grief eventually recedes, it doesn't leave the heart as it was before. It leaves a larger vessel. A heart that has been stretched by sorrow now has the volume to hold a much more profound "rapture" than it could have ever contained before.

People who have walked through deep valleys carry a different kind of presence. They possess a groundedness and a tenderness that only comes from being enlarged by what they survived. In the hierarchy of wholeness, this is the soul softening so the spirit can finally lead. They no longer fear the shaking, because they know they have the capacity to hold both the sorrow and the glory.

What Healing Hides Inside Defeat?

Defeat is not the end of the story; it is the moment the narrative changes direction. In our modern culture, we are taught to "pivot" or "hustle" our way out of defeat. But there is a sacred work that can only happen in the stillness of a loss.

When we are defeated, the ego cracks. The ego is the part of us that believes we are in total control, that our "performance" defines our worth, and that we are self-sufficient. When life brings us to our knees, that shell breaks, and the spirit finally has room to step forward.

Defeat serves us by:

  • Stripping away illusions: It removes the false belief that we can control every outcome.
  • Revealing what is real: In a loss, you quickly find out which friendships are true, which values are unshakable, and what truly remains when everything else is stripped away.
  • Forcing an inward listen: We usually ignore the spirit when things are going "well." Defeat creates a silence that forces us to listen to the deeper parts of our design.
  • Teaching the holy art of surrender: This is the shift from Self-Actualization to Christ-Actualization.

It is the moment we stop relying on our own fragile strength and begin to root ourselves in something deeper, wiser, and more eternal. It is the moment we realize that while we may have lost a "battle," we are being won by a much larger Truth.

What Is the Anatomy of Expansion?

"Sorrows come
To stretch out spaces in the heart for joy."

This is the mechanics of healing: Pain stretches. Stretching expands. Expansion creates capacity. Think of a balloon being inflated for the first time. The rubber is tight and resistant. But once it has been stretched, it can hold more air with less effort. Healing is not about erasing the wound or pretending the defeat didn't happen. It is about enlarging the heart around the wound. Researchers who study post-traumatic growth describe a similar pattern: profound struggle can precede profound growth.

The scar becomes part of the architecture of your strength. You become a person of "Great Capacity." You can hold the weight of others' pain because you have already been stretched. You can appreciate the smallest flicker of light because you have known the weight of the dark. This capacity is exactly what makes room for truth, for God, and for a joy that isn't dependent on circumstances.

What Does This Mean for Your Journey?

If you are in a season of shaking, stretching, or straining, please know this: You are not failing. You are being formed.

In the Hierarchy of Wholeness, you are currently moving through the "Shaking of the Soul" so that the Spirit can take its rightful place at the top.

  1. Your Body is learning to find calm even when the wind is blowing.
  2. Your Soul is being stretched to hold a larger capacity for life.
  3. Your Spirit is being revealed as the ego-shell cracks open.
The storm is not here to destroy you; it is here to reveal you. It is here to "let the glory out."

When the wind finally passes, and it will pass, you will not be the same tree. You will be stronger, steadier, and more alive than ever before. You will find that your roots have found a deeper anchor, and your branches have a new reach. This is the hidden miracle of the Renewal journey: What once felt like defeat becomes the very doorway that lets the glory out.

If the shaking season has left your body braced and your nervous system on high alert, you do not have to walk it alone. Read about emotional overwhelm and nervous system support, explore working together, or get in touch whenever you are ready.

Reflection for the Shaken Soul

  1. Identify the Strain: In what area of your life does the "wind" feel strongest right now? Can you reframe that strain as "stress wood" being built?
  2. Look for the Root: How is this pressure forcing you to anchor yourself in something deeper than your own strength?
  3. Honor the Space: Can you visualize your current sorrow as a "space-maker"? If your heart is being stretched right now, what beautiful thing might it be preparing to hold?
