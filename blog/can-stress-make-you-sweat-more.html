Can Stress Make You Sweat More? Why It Happens

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 31, 2026

Yes, stress can make you sweat more. When your nervous system senses pressure, it switches on your body's alarm response, and sweat glands are part of that response. Damp palms, sweaty underarms, or a sudden flush are common. Calming your nervous system and checking other causes with your doctor both help.

Why do I sweat when I am stressed?

Your body was designed with a beautiful alarm system. When it senses a challenge, your sympathetic nervous system steps forward to get you ready. Your heart speeds up, your breath quickens, your muscles prepare to move, and your sweat glands begin to work. In a true emergency, that sweat helps cool a body that is about to run or fight.

The trouble is that your nervous system cannot always tell the difference between a lion and a tense meeting. A hard phone call, a crowded room, a deadline, or speaking in front of others can all trigger the same response. So the sweat arrives, even though you are only sitting at a desk.

Stress sweat often shows up in the palms, the soles of the feet, the underarms, and the face. It can feel embarrassing, but it is not a character flaw. It is your body doing its job a little too faithfully.

Is stress sweat different from regular sweat?

Many people notice that stress sweat feels different, and there is a reason. You have more than one kind of sweat gland. The glands that cool you during exercise mostly release water and salt. Glands concentrated in places like the underarms also respond strongly to emotional stress, and their sweat contains more fats and proteins. When skin bacteria break those down, odor can follow.

That is why a nervous afternoon can leave you feeling sweatier and less fresh than an hour of yard work.

How do I stop sweating from anxiety?

You cannot command your sweat glands to stop. You can, however, send your nervous system signals that it is safe.

  1. Lengthen your exhale. Breathe in for a count of four and out for a count of six. A longer breath out is one of the clearest calming signals you can give your body.
  2. Cool the body gently. A cool drink, stepping into fresh air, or running cool water over your wrists can help.
  3. Dress for ease. Breathable fabrics and layers you can remove give you room to settle.
  4. Prepare, then pause. Before a moment you know will stir you up, take two minutes to ground yourself. My guide to grounding techniques for overwhelm walks through simple anchors.
  5. Watch the stimulants. Many people notice caffeine adds to both the racing heart and the sweat.

Over time, working with the stress underneath matters more than any single trick. A body that feels safer more often has fewer reasons to sound the alarm.

When should I worry about excessive sweating?

Please do not assume all sweating is stress. MedlinePlus explains that sweating can also come with fever, low blood sugar, an overactive thyroid, menopause, certain medications, and other conditions.

See your doctor if heavy sweating is new for you, happens without any trigger, soaks your clothes or bedding, or comes with weight loss, fever, or a racing heart at rest. Sweating at night has its own causes, which I explore in can stress cause night sweats. If you are noticing chills or feeling hot without a clear reason, my post on whether stress can cause a fever may help you sort out what to ask your doctor.

Seek emergency care right away if sweating comes with chest pain, shortness of breath, pain spreading into your arm or jaw, confusion, or fainting. Please never stop or change a prescribed medication on your own.

How can I calm the stress behind the sweat?

Sweat is a messenger, not the message. It tells you your nervous system has been working hard.

FAQ

Can anxiety cause sweating without being hot?

Yes. Anxiety activates the same alarm response that triggers sweat, so you can sweat in a cool room. If it happens often with no trigger, mention it to your doctor.

Why do my hands sweat when I am nervous?

Your palms have many sweat glands that respond strongly to emotional stress. Slow breathing and grounding before a stressful moment can help.

Is excessive sweating a sign of something serious?

Sometimes. Sweating that is new, unexplained, or paired with fever, weight loss, or chest pain deserves a doctor's evaluation.
