Why Healing Is Not a Straight Line

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · September 14, 2026

Healing is not linear because the body does not repair on a schedule. Progress in root-cause work tends to move in a spiral: you circle back through familiar symptoms and old emotions, but you meet them with more capacity each time. A return is not a reset. It is usually the next layer becoming visible.

Why does healing feel like going backwards?

Because you are meeting an old pattern again, and the meeting feels identical even when you are not.

Survival taught most of us urgency. It conditioned the nervous system to believe that safety comes from speed and from getting things right the first time. So when a familiar ache or a familiar fear resurfaces after a good stretch, the mind reaches for the harshest available explanation: it did not work, or worse, I did something wrong.

What is far more often true is that what was built over years, and sometimes over generations, does not come undone in an afternoon. The body releases in layers. It brings forward what it has capacity to handle, and it waits until it has that capacity. A symptom that returns after a quiet season is frequently the sign of a system with enough reserve to finally look at something it had to shelve.

Is revisiting an old symptom a sign of failure?

No. It is far more likely to be the spiral doing its work.

Picture a spiral staircase rather than a ladder. On a ladder, passing the same point twice means you slipped. On a spiral, you pass above the same point on every turn, seeing the same view from a different height. When you find yourself facing a fear you thought you had finished with, notice what is different: you named it faster, it frightened you less, you recovered in two days instead of two weeks. And when it is a routine that slipped rather than an old pattern resurfacing, here is how to get back on track without shame.

That is not the beginning again. You are meeting the pattern with new awareness, new tools, and a steadier spirit. Trust the return. Nothing in the process is wasted.

How long does root-cause work take?

Honestly, nobody can tell you that in advance, and anyone who gives you a confident number is selling you certainty rather than care.

The timeline depends on how long the pattern has been in place, what your body is carrying, how much genuine rest is available in your life right now, and what else is going on medically. That is not a dodge. It is the reason the work starts with your history rather than with a protocol.

What I can tell you is that this is a relationship rather than a task list. The long arc of healing is patient. It does not shame your pauses and it does not demand you rush. If you would like a sense of how that unfolds in practice, my root-cause services overview walks through what the work actually involves, and the free guide is a gentle place to begin.

What does progress look like when it is not linear?

Stop measuring only the absence of symptoms, which is the one number that moves last, and watch these instead.

Recovery time. The flare still happens, and you are back on your feet in a fraction of the time. This is usually the very first thing to shift.

Baseline between the hard days. The good days get quietly better. People often miss this one entirely because the bad days are louder.

Capacity. You handle a stressful week without the whole system collapsing. Your window has widened.

The internal voice. The storyteller in your soul stops narrating every setback as evidence against you. Self-criticism softens into curiosity.

Body signals you can read. You notice the bracing in your shoulders while it is happening, not three weeks later. Awareness arriving earlier is real progress, even when nothing else has changed yet.

Research groups including the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health now frame this as whole-person health, which looks at the whole system rather than one isolated part. The order matters too, and I wrote about it in body, soul, spirit and why the healing order matters.

How do you keep going through a plateau?

By changing what you ask of the plateau.

A flat stretch is not nothing happening. It is often consolidation, the body practising a new normal until it stops being new. Pushing harder here is the instinct, and it usually backfires, because the nervous system reads pressure as another emergency and chronic stress is the very thing you are trying to unwind.

Keep the rhythm small and keep it. Steady beats heroic. And if the flat stretch feels less like rest and more like waiting for permission to move, it may be a holding pattern rather than a plateau. If the plateau comes with real overwhelm rather than simple impatience, that is its own layer, and nervous system and emotional overwhelm support is where that work lives.

Pause for reflection

Place a hand on your heart and let one breath go all the way out.

Then ask gently. Where am I judging a return as failure? And what is actually different about how I am meeting it this time?

Psalm 23:3 says he restores my soul. Restoration is a process word, not an event word. You are not behind. You are being formed.

FAQ

Does a symptom coming back mean the approach is not working?

Not by itself. Look at the whole picture: how long it lasted, how hard it hit, how quickly you recovered. Those three usually shift before the symptom disappears. Any new, severe, or rapidly changing symptom should go to your physician rather than into a healing framework.

How do I know when to change course?

When nothing on the progress list above has moved over a genuinely fair stretch of time, and when your practitioner cannot explain why. A good practitioner welcomes that question instead of deflecting it.

Is it normal to feel worse before feeling better?

Some people describe a temporary wobble as the body adjusts. It should be mild and short. Anything intense, worsening, or frightening is a reason to stop and speak with your physician, not something to push through.
