What Is Body Doubling? Why Company Helps You Get Things Done

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · November 27, 2026

Body doubling means doing a task while another person is present, either in the room or on a video call. They do not have to help or even do the same thing. Their quiet company acts as an anchor that makes it easier to start, stay focused and finish. It is especially popular among people with ADHD.

What is body doubling, in plain English?

You may have done this without knowing it had a name. You folded the laundry while a friend sat on the bed and talked. You finally answered the emails because your sister was working across the table.

Cleveland Clinic describes body doubling as doing a task or project in the presence of one or more people, where the other person serves as an anchor that encourages focus and accountability. Behavioural health specialist Michael Manos, PhD, calls it a form of "external executive functioning." In other words, another person lends you a little of the structure your own brain is struggling to supply in that moment.

Why does body doubling work?

It seems backwards at first. Surely more people means more distraction?

Dr. Manos explains that the opposite is often true. If your brain is used to being pulled toward whatever pops up around you, then a person nearby who is quietly working gives it something steady to follow. In his words, modelled behaviour is very potent. He compares it to a parent sitting beside a child at homework time, which can be highly effective.

I would add one thing from my own work. We are made to settle in the presence of safe people. A calm person in the room tells your nervous system that you are not facing this pile alone, and a body that feels less alone has more to give the task. I describe that settling in what is co-regulation.

Who is body doubling for?

It is best known as a focus strategy for people with ADHD, a condition that affects the part of the brain that oversees executive function, including the ability to begin a task. If starting is your hardest step, you will find more on that in what is executive dysfunction.

You do not need a diagnosis to use it, and I do not diagnose. Cleveland Clinic notes that body doubling can help whether ADHD is affecting your focus or you are simply feeling overwhelmed by a task. It also works for adults of every age, not only children.

Needing company to get something done is not a weakness, and it is not laziness. Brains are wired differently, not incorrectly. Using a support that fits your wiring is wisdom.

How do you try body doubling?

Cleveland Clinic offers several simple ways:

  • In person. Ask a friend or family member to sit with you while you work. They can do their own task. Setting a regular time helps, because knowing someone expects you makes it easier to show up.
  • By video call. Keep a call open with a friend, coworker or study partner while each of you does your own work. It works best when cameras stay on.
  • In a quiet public place. A library or a calm coffee shop, where others are working, can create the same focused feeling, even among strangers.
  • In an online community. There are online spaces built for this, where people work quietly alongside one another.

Before you begin, tell your partner the one thing you plan to do. Saying it aloud turns a vague weight into a single step.

How long should a body doubling session be?

There is no perfect length. Cleveland Clinic suggests matching the session to the task, and it notes that a short time commitment can make it easier to start when motivation is low. Longer sessions suit bigger projects, as long as you rest afterwards.

Build in breaks. You might work for a while, then stop for lunch or easy conversation together. A small shared reward gives both of you something to look forward to.

A gentle practice: choose one task you have been avoiding this week. Text one safe person and ask, "Could you sit with me, or stay on a call, while I do this?" You may be surprised how often the answer is, "I need that too."

When should you ask for more support?

Body doubling is a helpful tool. It is not a treatment, and it will not suit every person or every task. If trouble with focus, starting or finishing is affecting your work, your home or your relationships, please talk with your physician or a qualified mental health professional. They can look at the whole picture and discuss options with you.

In my practice, neurodivergent support is about calming the system and honouring the person, never fixing them. You can see how that sits within the rest of my root-cause services, always alongside your medical team's care.

"Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour" (Ecclesiastes 4:9, KJV). You were never designed to do it all by yourself.

FAQ

Does a body double have to help with the task?

No. Cleveland Clinic explains that the other person can simply sit nearby or work on their own task. Their presence is what helps.

Can body doubling work online?

Yes. A video call with a friend, coworker or study partner can work well, especially when everyone keeps their camera on.

Is body doubling only for people with ADHD?

No. It is best known as an ADHD focus strategy, but Cleveland Clinic notes it can also help anyone who feels overwhelmed by a task.
