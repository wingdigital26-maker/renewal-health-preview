Why Does My Energy Crash Every Afternoon?

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · September 20, 2026

An afternoon energy crash usually reflects three things acting together: your natural cortisol rhythm falling, blood sugar swinging after lunch, and cellular energy production running low. It is a pattern, not a character flaw. The most useful changes happen in the morning, several hours before the crash arrives.

What is actually happening at two o'clock?

Some of it is simply design. Human alertness dips in the early afternoon in most people, and that part does not need fixing.

What is not ordinary is the version that flattens you. The eyes go heavy, the words stop connecting, and your body starts negotiating for sugar. That is a body running low on usable fuel, and three contributors usually drive it.

Does cortisol cause the afternoon slump?

It is part of it. Cortisol is not only a stress hormone, it is also your daily get-up-and-go signal, and it follows a curve. It rises in the early morning, peaks shortly after you wake, and tapers across the day so you can eventually sleep.

When that curve is healthy, the afternoon taper is gentle. When your system has run in protection mode for years, the curve flattens or shifts. You wake already depleted, run on adrenaline until lunch, then fall off a cliff. If your whole day feels like that, wired but tired describes the state more fully, and cortisol and stubborn belly weight covers what the same pattern does to the middle of the body.

How does lunch change my afternoon?

More than almost anything else on this list.

A lunch that is mostly refined carbohydrate sends blood glucose up quickly. Insulin answers, and glucose often comes down further and faster than you would like. That downswing is the heaviness you feel. Then comes the craving, your body asking for the fastest correction, which sets up the next swing.

The fix is unglamorous and works: build lunch around protein, add fat and fibre, and let the starch be the smaller part of the plate. Eat sitting down, not standing at the counter with your phone. Digestion runs on the rest-and-digest branch of the nervous system, and a braced body does not digest well. That is the whole subject of why rest and digest matters for your gut.

If you have been told your blood sugar sits in a borderline range, that deserves a proper conversation with your physician. The National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases explains insulin resistance and prediabetes in plain language.

What do my cells have to do with it?

Inside nearly every cell sit the mitochondria, the small structures that turn food and oxygen into usable energy. When they run below par, the whole system feels it first as fatigue, because energy production is the cost of everything else.

Chronic stress is hard on this machinery. So is poor sleep, nutrient depletion, and a diet high in inflammatory load. Magnesium, B vitamins, and omega-3 fatty acids are foundational here, which is part of why a long season of stress and skipped meals shows up as an afternoon you cannot get through. Your cells are not being dramatic. They are accounting.

Please do not start a stack of supplements on the strength of a blog post, including mine. Nutrient needs are individual, some supplements interact with medication, and guessing is expensive in both directions.

What should I rule out with my physician first?

Persistent fatigue deserves a proper look before you adjust anything. Daily exhaustion has common physical contributors that are straightforward to check, and it is worth knowing which apply to you.

Ask your physician about:

  • Thyroid function, including a full panel rather than a single marker
  • Iron studies and a complete blood count, since low iron is common and easy to miss
  • Vitamin D and vitamin B12
  • Blood glucose and HbA1c
  • Sleep quality, including whether a sleep study is appropriate if you snore or wake unrefreshed
  • A review of your current medications, since fatigue is a common side effect

If you sleep a full night and still wake exhausted, waking up tired after a full night of sleep looks at that specific pattern.

What actually helps, starting tomorrow morning?

Get morning light. Ten to fifteen minutes of daylight early helps anchor the cortisol curve and the sleep signal at the other end of the day.

Eat a real breakfast with protein. Skipping it and running on coffee borrows energy you repay at two o'clock.

Move after lunch. A short walk blunts the glucose swing and gets the lymph and breath moving. Supporting your lymphatic system explains why movement matters more than it seems.

Take the dip rather than fight it. Ten quiet minutes, eyes closed, breathing low into the belly. A real pause costs less than the hour you lose pushing through.

Protect the evening. Late screens and late meals push the whole curve later, and tomorrow starts in a hole.

Rest is not a reward you earn after productivity. It is a regulatory necessity. "They that wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength" (Isaiah 40:31). The strength is renewed, not manufactured. Where hormonal rhythm is the driver, hormone balance support is where this work lives in my practice.

Pause for reflection

Sit back and let your breath drop from your chest into your belly. Three slow rounds, nothing forced.

Ask yourself gently: what am I asking my body to run on today? And what would change if I treated the afternoon dip as information rather than a failure of will?

Your energy is not gone. It is being spent somewhere you have not looked yet.

FAQ

Is an afternoon energy crash normal?

A mild early-afternoon dip is common and expected. A crash that regularly stops you functioning is worth investigating with your physician.

Will more coffee fix it?

It will postpone it. Caffeine later in the day also tends to shorten deep sleep, which makes tomorrow's crash deeper.

Should I nap?

A short rest of about twenty minutes can help. Longer naps late in the day often make night sleep harder.

Where should I begin?

Begin with breakfast, daylight, and a physician visit if the fatigue is persistent. When you would like support shaped around your own history, you can start a conversation through the contact page.
