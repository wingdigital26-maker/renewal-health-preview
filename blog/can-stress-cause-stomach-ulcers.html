Can Stress Cause Stomach Ulcers? What Science Says

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 31, 2026

Stress alone is not considered the main cause of most stomach ulcers. Research points to a bacterial infection called H. pylori and regular use of certain pain relievers as the usual culprits. Stress can still make a sensitive stomach feel worse, so caring for your nervous system matters alongside your doctor's care.

Can stress really cause a stomach ulcer?

For a long time, people believed ulcers came from worry, spicy food, and a hard-driving personality. Then science changed the story. According to the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases, the most common causes of peptic ulcers are infection with Helicobacter pylori and long-term use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, the over-the-counter pain relievers many of us keep in a kitchen drawer.

So no, you did not worry yourself into an ulcer. That matters, because shame never heals anything.

There is one exception worth knowing. People who are critically ill in a hospital can develop what doctors call stress ulcers, from the intense physical strain of severe illness or injury. That is a medical situation handled by a hospital team, and it is different from the everyday stress of a busy life.

Why does my stomach hurt more when I am stressed?

Here is where stress does come back into the picture. Your gut and your brain are in constant conversation. When your nervous system senses pressure, it shifts energy away from digestion and toward survival. Blood flow, muscle movement in the gut, and even how sensitive your stomach feels to discomfort can all change.

That means stress can make existing stomach trouble feel louder. A burning ache you might barely notice on a calm day can feel sharp on a frantic one. Your stomach is not lying to you. It is a messenger, telling you the load has been heavy.

If stomach discomfort is a regular visitor for you, my post on whether stress can cause stomach pain looks at the gut-brain connection in more depth. And if your mouth is where the sores show up, a canker sore is a very different kind of ulcer, which I cover in can stress cause canker sores.

What are the signs of a stomach ulcer?

Only a doctor can tell you whether you have an ulcer. Common signs people describe include:

  • A burning or gnawing ache in the upper belly, often between meals or at night
  • Bloating or fullness soon after eating
  • Nausea or frequent burping
  • Pain that eases briefly with food or antacids, then returns

These signs overlap with acid reflux, gallbladder trouble, and other conditions, which is exactly why guessing at home is not enough.

When should I see a doctor about stomach pain?

Please do not wait on stomach pain that keeps returning. Make an appointment if the burning lasts more than a few days, if you rely on antacids most days, if you have lost weight without trying, or if eating has become difficult.

Seek emergency care right away if you vomit blood or something that looks like coffee grounds, if your stools are black, tarry, or bloody, if you suddenly feel a sharp, severe pain in your belly that does not let up, or if you feel faint or dizzy along with stomach pain.

If you take pain relievers regularly, talk to your doctor or pharmacist about them. Please never stop or change a prescribed medication on your own. Natural-first support walks alongside your doctor, never instead of them.

How can I support a stressed stomach naturally?

Once your doctor has looked at the cause, there is gentle, everyday care that helps your gut feel safe enough to do its work.

  1. Eat slowly and sit down. Digestion begins in a calm body. Eating while rushing, driving, or scrolling keeps you in survival mode.
  2. Breathe before you bite. A few slow breaths with a longer exhale tell your nervous system it is safe to rest and digest.
  3. Notice your triggers. Many people find alcohol, smoking, and very large late meals make their stomach feel worse. Your doctor can guide you on what fits your situation.
  4. Rest on purpose. Sleep and real pauses in the day give your whole system room to repair.

FAQ

Can anxiety cause ulcers?

Anxiety is not considered a main cause of ulcers. It can, however, make stomach symptoms feel stronger, so caring for anxiety and seeing your doctor about the symptoms both matter.

What is the most common cause of stomach ulcers?

The most common causes are H. pylori infection and regular use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory pain relievers. A doctor can test for H. pylori and review your medications.

Can stress make an existing ulcer worse?

Stress can make digestive discomfort feel more intense. Follow your doctor's treatment plan, and think of calming your nervous system as support, not a substitute.
