What Is Holistic Health? A Plain-English Guide

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · November 10, 2026

Holistic health is an approach to wellbeing that looks at the whole person: body, mind, emotions, relationships, and spirit, rather than one symptom or one organ at a time. It asks what is happening underneath a symptom and works alongside conventional medical care, never in place of it. It is educational, gentle, and personal.

What does holistic health actually mean?

The word holistic comes from "whole." The idea is simple. You are not a collection of separate parts that happen to share a body. Your sleep affects your mood, your mood affects your digestion, and your digestion affects your energy. A holistic view notices those threads.

The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health describes whole person health as helping people improve and restore their health across connected areas, biological, behavioral, social, and environmental, rather than just focusing on disease. I find that a helpful, grounded place to start.

How is holistic health different from conventional medicine?

Conventional medicine is excellent at diagnosis, emergencies, infections, surgery, and prescriptions. I am grateful for it, and I always want my clients to have a physician they trust.

Holistic care asks a different set of questions. Not only "what is the symptom?" but "what is the terrain this symptom grew in?" How are you sleeping? What have the last few years asked of you? Does your body ever get a chance to feel safe? I like to say symptoms are messengers. A holistic approach slows down long enough to read the message.

Is holistic health the same as alternative medicine?

Not quite, and the difference matters. NCCIH explains that "alternative" means using a non-mainstream approach instead of conventional care, while "integrative" means bringing conventional and complementary approaches together in a coordinated way.

My work is natural-first, never "instead of." Please keep seeing your doctor, keep taking any prescribed medication as directed, and talk with your physician before changing anything. Good holistic care should make that relationship stronger, not replace it.

What does a holistic health practitioner do?

Practitioners vary a great deal, so it is worth asking anyone about their training. I am a Holistic Health Practitioner, a Registered Naturopath, and a Clinical Homeopathic Healthcare Practitioner, and I bring those together in what I call root-cause care.

In practice, that means listening first. We look at nutrition, digestion, stress, sleep, and the stories your body has been carrying. Then we build gentle, sustainable steps, often starting with the nervous system, because a body that feels hunted cannot rest, digest, or repair well. If you are curious how the two main disciplines I use differ, I wrote about naturopath vs homeopath, and about what a naturopath does.

Why does the mind-body connection matter?

Because your body keeps score of what your mind is carrying, and your mind is shaped by what your body feels. Long seasons of stress can show up as tension, poor sleep, cravings, or a gut that never quite settles. None of that means it is "all in your head." It means your whole self is talking.

I go deeper into this in mind-body integration, the missing link in healing. The short version: when body, soul, and spirit start moving in the same direction, healing has room to breathe.

How do you start with holistic health?

Start small. You do not need a cabinet full of supplements or a perfect plan.

  • Notice your patterns. Jot down sleep, energy, digestion, and mood for a week.
  • Offer your body one safety signal a day. A slow exhale, a short walk outside, or a meal eaten sitting down.
  • Bring your notes to your physician. Rule out anything that needs medical attention first.
  • Find a practitioner who listens. You should feel heard, never rushed or shamed.

FAQ

Is holistic health evidence-based?

Some holistic practices have growing research behind them, and others have very little. A good practitioner will be honest about that and will encourage you to keep your physician involved. NCCIH is a reliable place to read about the evidence for specific approaches.

Can holistic health replace my doctor?

No. Holistic care is meant to work alongside conventional care. Please keep your physician involved, keep taking prescribed medication as directed, and talk with them before making changes.

What is the difference between holistic and integrative health?

Holistic describes the whole-person view. Integrative describes bringing conventional and complementary care together in a coordinated way. Many practitioners, including me, aim for both.
