Why Can't I Relax on Vacation? When Your Body Will Not Clock Out

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 28, 2026

Many people cannot relax on vacation because their nervous system has been running in high alert for so long that stopping feels unfamiliar, even unsafe. The body does not switch off just because the calendar says "day off." It needs time and gentle signals of safety to downshift. Restlessness on a break is not ingratitude. It is a body still bracing.

Why can't I relax even when I have time off?

Think of a car that has been driven fast on the highway for months. When you finally take the exit, the engine is still hot and humming. Your nervous system works much the same way. After a long season of deadlines, caregiving, or worry, the stress response becomes the default setting. When the pressure suddenly lifts, your body does not trust the quiet yet.

That is why the first days of a trip can feel strangely tense. You check your phone. You plan the next activity. You lie on a beach chair and your mind runs through every email you did not answer. I call this the sympathetic lock: the body stays braced even after the danger has passed, because bracing is what it knows.

For many people there is also an old inner rule underneath, something like "It is not safe to rest" or "I have to earn my break." If rest brings up guilt, you may find words for it in my post on feeling guilty for resting.

Why do I get sick or feel worse when I finally stop?

Some people notice that they come down with a cold, a headache, or a wave of exhaustion on the first day of vacation. Researchers have described this pattern informally as "leisure sickness." It is not fully understood, and it is not a diagnosis, but one common explanation is that stress hormones can keep symptoms muted while you push through, and when you stop, your body finally lets you feel what it has been carrying.

Tiredness that hits when you slow down is often the body catching up, not the body failing. Some people even notice feeling feverish or flushed in these moments. My post on whether stress can cause a fever explains what is known about stress and body temperature, and when a fever needs a doctor.

Is it normal to feel anxious on vacation?

Yes, it is very common. Unstructured time removes the busyness that usually keeps feelings at bay. When there is nothing to do, what you have been too busy to feel can rise to the surface: grief, loneliness, fatigue, or a quiet sense that life is out of alignment. That can feel like anxiety.

This does not mean vacations are bad for you. It means your body is using the space to speak. Listening to it, rather than filling every hour, is part of healing.

How do I actually relax on vacation?

You cannot force your body to relax, but you can make it easier for it to land.

  1. Plan a landing day. Give yourself one slow day at the start with no plans. Your body needs time to downshift before it can enjoy anything.
  2. Lengthen your exhale. Breathe in for a count of four and out for a count of six, a few times a day. A longer exhale is one of the simplest signals of safety you can give your nervous system.
  3. Set gentle phone boundaries. Choose one short window to check messages, then put the phone away. Constant checking keeps the body on duty.
  4. Move your body softly. Walking, swimming, or stretching help burn off stored stress energy so stillness feels less jarring.
  5. Lower the pressure to have fun. You do not need a perfect trip. Rest that looks quiet and ordinary still counts.

If you notice this pattern everywhere, not just on vacation, it may be a sign of living in survival mode. My post on how to get out of survival mode goes deeper. And if you would like to understand what sits underneath your stress, my root-cause services look at body, soul, and spirit together.

When should I talk to a doctor?

Please speak with your physician if you feel constantly exhausted, unwell, or unable to rest for weeks at a time, or if anxiety is affecting your sleep, appetite, or daily life. The NIMH fact sheet on stress describes signs that stress may need professional support. If you ever have thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 in the US, or call 911.

Pause for Reflection

Sit somewhere comfortable. Let your feet rest on the ground and your shoulders soften.

Ask gently: What is my body still bracing for? What would it feel like to let myself be off duty, even for one hour?

"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28). Rest is not something you earn at the end of being good enough. It is an invitation you are allowed to accept today.

FAQ

How long does it take to relax on vacation?

It varies. Many people find it takes a few days for the body to downshift after a busy season. A slow first day and gentle routines can help that transition.

Why do I feel restless when I have nothing to do?

Restlessness often means the nervous system is used to constant activity. Without a task, the body may still be producing energy for action. Gentle movement and slow breathing can help it settle.

Is it bad that I do not enjoy vacations?

No. Many people find breaks hard at first, especially after long stress. It is information about your body, not a failure. With practice, rest usually becomes easier to receive.
