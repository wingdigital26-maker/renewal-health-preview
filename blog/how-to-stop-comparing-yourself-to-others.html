How to Stop Comparing Yourself to Others

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 6, 2026

To stop comparing yourself to others, first notice the moment it starts and how your body reacts. Then calm that reaction, remind yourself that you are seeing someone else's highlights, and return your attention to your own next step. Comparison fades when you feel safe in your own life, not when you finally win the race.

Why do I compare myself to others so much?

Comparison is very human. Psychologists have long described it as one of the ways we size ourselves up, and the American Psychological Association defines social comparison as judging our own abilities and worth against other people.

Underneath, there is something older going on. For most of history, belonging to the group meant survival. So when you see someone who seems further ahead, your nervous system can quietly ask, "Am I going to be left behind?" That is why comparison does not feel like a thought. It feels like a drop in the stomach, a tightening in the chest, a sudden heaviness.

Comparison is not vanity. It is your body checking whether you are still safe.

Why does comparison make me feel so bad?

Because you are comparing your inside to someone else's outside. You know every doubt, every hard morning, every unfinished thing in your own life. You see only the polished moments in theirs. It is never a fair measurement.

Over time, constant comparison keeps the body in a low hum of threat. You may notice restlessness, trouble resting, or a feeling that you are always behind. That inner voice saying "not enough" is often a familiar one, and my post on how to change negative self-talk explores where it comes from.

How do I stop comparing myself on social media?

Start small and kind. You do not have to delete everything at once.

  1. Notice the drop. When you feel that sinking after scrolling, pause and name it: "I am comparing again."
  2. Breathe before you judge. Take two slow breaths with a longer out-breath. Let your body settle before you decide anything about your life.
  3. Curate gently. Mute or unfollow accounts that leave you feeling smaller, even if they are lovely people.
  4. Set a soft boundary. Keep your phone out of reach for the first and last hour of the day, when you are most tender.
  5. Turn envy into information. Ask, "What does this show me that I long for?" Sometimes comparison points to a desire worth honoring.

How do I accept my own pace in life?

By remembering that your life was never meant to run on someone else's timeline. Seasons are different. Callings are different. Bodies are different. Some of the most meaningful growth happens in quiet places that no one else ever sees.

I think of this as returning to your Original Design, the person you were made to be before you learned to measure yourself. Confidence grows from that place, not from being ahead of anyone. My post on how to build self-confidence goes deeper into what steady, rooted confidence looks like.

Can comparison affect your health?

It can add to the stress your body carries. When the nervous system feels it must keep proving itself, it rarely fully rests. That steady pressure can show up in sleep, digestion, energy, and mood.

This is part of why I look at the whole person rather than one symptom. You can see the different ways I support clients on my services page, and if you want a structured path over time, my programs and packages explain how that work is organized. Every season of support starts with the same question: what does your body need to feel safe in its own life?

When should you talk to someone about comparison?

If comparison leads to persistent sadness, anxiety, or thoughts that you are worthless, or if it affects your eating, sleep, or relationships, please speak with your physician or a licensed mental health professional. You deserve real support. If you ever have thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 in the US.

Pause for Reflection

Put your phone down for a moment. Place a hand on your heart and take a slow breath.

Ask yourself: Whose life have I been measuring mine against? What is one thing in my own life, right now, that is quietly good?

"Each one should test their own actions. Then they can take pride in themselves alone, without comparing themselves to someone else" (Galatians 6:4, NIV). You were never meant to run someone else's race.

You are not behind. You are on your own path, and it is enough.

FAQ

Is it normal to compare yourself to others?

Yes. Comparison is a very human habit. It becomes a problem when it leaves you feeling constantly behind or unworthy.

Does comparison ever help?

Sometimes. A little comparison can show you what you value or long for. The aim is to learn from it without letting it define your worth.

How long does it take to stop comparing?

It is usually a gradual shift. Each time you notice it and return to your own path, the habit loosens a little.
