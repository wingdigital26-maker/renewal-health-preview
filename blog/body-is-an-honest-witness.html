Your Body Is the Most Honest Witness You Have

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · September 7, 2026

Your body has never lied to you. Every ache, every wave of fatigue, every stomach that tightens under pressure is a report rather than a malfunction. Learning to read that report instead of silencing it is usually where feeling better actually begins, and it starts with taking your own signals seriously.

What is your body actually trying to say?

Your body does not have words, so it uses sensation instead. Tightness, exhaustion, a racing heart, a stomach that will not settle. These are not random glitches in the machinery. They are your body's best available attempt to tell you that something changed, something is under strain, or something has been asked of it for too long.

This is one of the most important shifts a person can make: treating a symptom as a message worth reading rather than a nuisance to shut up as quickly as possible. It does not mean every twinge carries a hidden meaning. It means that dismissing a pattern that keeps repeating closes off information your body is actively handing you.

Why do symptoms get written off as overreacting?

Most of us learned early to push through. Tiredness got read as weakness. Pain got treated as an inconvenience to work around rather than a signal to slow down for. Over years, that becomes a habit of overriding your own testimony so consistently that you genuinely cannot hear it anymore.

Here is the trouble. A body that is repeatedly overridden does not go quiet. It gets louder. It shows up in new places, at less convenient times, in ways that are harder to ignore, until the message finally lands. Looking underneath a symptom instead of only managing around it is the whole premise of root cause work.

What does this look like in ordinary life?

  • Fatigue that does not lift no matter how much you sleep
  • A stomach that tightens predictably around certain people or certain weeks
  • Headaches that arrive after a run of overwhelming days
  • Skin that flares in high pressure seasons and settles when things ease
  • Months of being tired and pushing through it anyway

None of these prove any one cause on their own, and anyone who tells you they do is guessing. They are patterns worth paying attention to rather than patterns worth outrunning.

How do you start listening instead of overriding?

Start small and start specific. When a symptom shows up, notice what the hours and days before it looked like. Name what you feel in plain language instead of shrinking it, because "I am exhausted" is usable information and "I am fine, just a bit tired" is not.

Then change the question. Most of us ask how to make the sensation stop. Ask instead what your body might be asking for. More rest. Less pressure. Different food. A season with fewer things in it. Support you have not let yourself accept yet.

Looking at the whole pattern a body is showing, rather than treating each symptom as an unrelated event, is exactly what a naturopathic and homeopathic approach is built to do.

When should you loop in your doctor?

Anything severe, sudden, or persistent deserves a conversation with your physician, no matter how much of this article rings true for you. Your body's testimony is genuinely useful information, and it works best carried into your doctor's office rather than kept out of it. For general background on how symptoms are evaluated, the Cleveland Clinic health library is a reliable independent resource.

Pause for reflection

Psalm 139 says we are fearfully and wonderfully made. Something built with that much care is worth listening to rather than managing around.

Your body has been telling you something for a while now. It might be time to stop calling it an inconvenience and start calling it a witness.

FAQ

Is it normal not to notice my symptoms until they are severe?

Yes, especially if you have spent years overriding the smaller ones. Noticing earlier is a skill, and like any skill it builds with practice rather than arriving all at once.

Does listening to my body mean ignoring medical advice?

Not at all. The two are not in competition. Your own observations are useful information to bring into your doctor's office, not a reason to stay out of it.

What if I cannot tell what my body is trying to say?

That is common and it is not a failure on your part. Individual symptoms are hard to interpret alone. Working with someone who looks at the whole pattern often makes sense of what felt like unrelated noise. The wider lens behind this idea is in what shalom means for your body.

Is this a replacement for my doctor's care?

No. This is educational support for everyday wellness. It is not medical advice and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease. Anything unexplained or persistent belongs with your physician.
