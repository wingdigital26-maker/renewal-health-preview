Why Do I Get Anxious on Sunday Nights? The Sunday Scaries

By Lynette Wing, Clinical Homeopath, Naturopath, Mind-Body Practitioner · October 16, 2026

Many people get anxious on Sunday nights because the body starts preparing for the week before the weekend is over. Your nervous system anticipates Monday's demands, deadlines, and pressure, and shifts toward alert early. That anticipatory stress, often called the Sunday scaries, is common and is usually a message about load, not a sign that something is wrong with you.

What are the Sunday scaries?

The Sunday scaries are the dread, restlessness, or low mood that creeps in as the weekend winds down. It is not a medical diagnosis. It is a familiar human experience: a tight chest at dinner, a mind already scrolling through Monday's list, trouble falling asleep, or a heaviness you cannot quite name.

Your body is wonderfully good at prediction. It learns patterns and gets ready for them. If Mondays have meant rushing, pressure, or walking into a place where you do not feel safe, your nervous system will start bracing hours ahead, the way a security guard checks the doors before anyone has knocked.

Why does anxiety get worse on Sunday evening?

A few common threads tend to weave together.

  • Anticipation. The mind rehearses what is coming. The body responds to that rehearsal almost as if it were already happening.
  • An unfinished week. Open loops from Friday, the unanswered email or the conversation you avoided, rise to the surface when things get quiet.
  • Rest that did not land. If the weekend was filled with errands and catching up, your system never fully left go mode, so Sunday feels like going back without a break.
  • Disrupted rhythm. Later nights and later mornings on the weekend can make Sunday sleep harder, which feeds the worry.
  • A deeper mismatch. Sometimes the dread is pointing at something real, a role, a pace, or a relationship that is costing more than it gives.

If your days run at a constant sprint, my post on hurry sickness explores that pattern. If the anxiety follows you into the next morning, read why you might wake up with anxiety.

Is it normal to dread Monday every week?

It is very common. Most people feel a little pre-week tension. What matters is the size of it and whether it is growing. A few minutes of tension that eases once Monday begins is different from dread that steals your whole Sunday, ruins sleep, or shows up every night of the week.

The National Institute of Mental Health overview of anxiety is a trustworthy read on when worry becomes something to talk with a professional about.

This is not weakness. Tenderness is information. Your body is telling you something about the load you are carrying into each week.

How can I stop feeling anxious on Sunday nights?

Think of these as gentle signals of safety that help your body cross from weekend into week without bracing.

  1. Close the loops on Friday. Spend ten minutes writing down what is unfinished and what comes first Monday. A list on paper lets the mind stop holding it.
  2. Give Sunday an anchor. A slow walk, a shared meal, worship, or a warm bath at the same time each week becomes a cue that says "we are safe here."
  3. Keep a steady bedtime. Going to bed near your usual weekday time helps the body clock settle.
  4. Plan one good thing for Monday. A favorite breakfast, a call with a friend, or a walk at lunch gives the week something to look forward to.
  5. Breathe the day out. A few minutes of slow breathing with a long exhale invites the rest-and-digest system to take the lead.
  6. Listen to the dread. Ask what, specifically, you are bracing against. The answer often points to a boundary or change that needs your attention.

In my emotional overwhelm and nervous system support, I help people understand what their body is bracing against and build rhythms that let it soften. My book, Nothing Missing, Nothing Broken, goes deeper into how the body, soul, and spirit learn safety again.

When should I get support for weekly anxiety?

Please talk with your physician or a licensed mental health professional if dread is spreading beyond Sundays, if you cannot sleep most nights, if you are having panic attacks, or if anxiety is making it hard to work, eat, or connect with the people you love.

If you are having thoughts of harming yourself or not wanting to be here, please call or text 988 to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline right away. Natural-first support works best alongside your medical and mental health care, never instead of it.

Pause for Reflection

Sit somewhere quiet on Sunday evening. Place one hand on your heart and let your exhale lengthen.

Ask gently: What am I bracing for this week? Is that fear about something that is happening, or something that might? What is one thing I could set down tonight and pick up only if Monday truly needs it?

"Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof" (Matthew 6:34). Tonight only asks you to be here. Monday will have its own grace.

FAQ

Are the Sunday scaries a sign I should quit my job?

Not necessarily. Many people feel some Sunday tension in jobs they enjoy. But if the dread is heavy and constant, it is worth listening to what it is pointing at, perhaps with a trusted counselor.

Why do I feel anxious on Sundays even when I do not work Mondays?

The body can carry old patterns. School years, past jobs, or family routines can teach the nervous system to brace on Sunday evening long after life has changed.
